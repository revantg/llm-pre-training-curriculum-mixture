## Verdict for our assignment

**High value, and on the one V5 number the other five papers cannot touch: the long-context lane.** It is the only paper here that measures long-context capability as a function of *pretraining sequence-length share*, and the answer is a threshold rather than a gradient — multi-doc QA is ≈0 until pretraining contains sequences longer than the eval context, then jumps to near-saturation and stops improving (Fig. 5a: MDQA-10 = 0.8 → 20.8 → 21.2 → 20.4 for pretrain lengths 1k→2k→4k→8k) — while *reordering the same tokens* buys +5.0 MDQA-30 free (Table 2) and RoPE θ 10k→100k buys +5.4 MDQA free (Table 4). The consequence for our spec is that **a 6% long-context lane is defensible as a floor but is not where the win comes from and is not "extra" data**: RefinedWeb's own decomposition already puts **22.9% of tokens in ≥4k single-document sequences and 9.4% at exactly 8192** (Table 1, Natural), and raising the 8k share to 25% scores no better (22.1 vs 21.9, inside noise). Two hard limits on citing it: everything is **≤8k context on 160M–7B models** with Tables 1/2/5 **single-seed** against a self-reported MDQA std of ~1.6, and the 6× headline is a 410M number whose compute half largely evaporates at our scale (validated arithmetic: attention is 25.0% of FLOPs at d=2048/s=8192 but only **5.3%** at d=12288/s=8192).

## Teach this first

**1. The default way we make training data quietly destroys documents.** *Concat-and-chunk* = shuffle all tokenized documents, glue them end-to-end with an `<EOT>` separator, then slice the resulting river of tokens into equal blocks of the target length (2048 for Llama-1, 4096 for Llama-2). It is used because it wastes zero tokens and needs zero thought. But a document that straddles a block boundary gets cut in half even when it would have fit, so the average contiguous chunk is *shorter than the average document*: RefinedWeb documents average 598 tokens, but concat-and-chunk at 2k yields chunks averaging **463** (Fig. 3a). You paid for coherent documents and trained on fragments. *Why it matters for V5:* our packing choice silently sets the length distribution the model actually experiences, which — per this paper — is a first-order determinant of long-context skill. Packing is a mixture decision, not an infra detail.

**2. Dataset decomposition (DD) re-cuts instead of gluing.** Take each document of length `l`, write `l` in binary, and split it into pieces of exactly those power-of-two sizes. A 1,300-token document (1024+256+16+4) becomes four sequences landing in buckets D10, D8, D4, D2. Bucket `Dᵢ` holds only sequences of length exactly 2ⁱ, and every sequence inside it comes from **one** document. Three properties fall out for free: no cross-document attention *by construction* (no masking needed), every bucket is trivially batchable, and no token is thrown away or duplicated. Cost of the transform is negligible and it is per-document, so it drops into any existing tokenizer pipeline. *Why it matters for V5:* this is a cheap change to our data-prep stage that strictly dominates concat-and-chunk on step time, prep cost, and accuracy (Table 5), and it's the prerequisite for controlling length as a knob at all.

**3. Variable sequence length (VSL) keeps the optimizer's world unchanged.** At each step, pick a bucket `i`, then draw `b/2ⁱ` sequences from it. Because bucket sequences all have length 2ⁱ, tokens-per-step is always `b` no matter which bucket you picked. Long-sequence steps have few sequences, short-sequence steps have many. So batch-in-tokens, LR schedule, and warmup are untouched — the paper reuses the baseline's hyperparameters verbatim and never tunes for DD. *Why it matters for V5:* a length curriculum is one of the very few curriculum knobs that does **not** require re-tuning the run. It is nearly free to try and cannot be blamed for an LR-induced divergence.

**4. Where the speed comes from, and why it will shrink for us.** Attention is quadratic in sequence length, so at fixed tokens-per-step a long-sequence step costs more. Measured on 8×H100 with FSDP+FlashAttention2 at 8192 tokens/GPU, one OpenLM-1B step costs 224 ms at length 64 but 304 ms at length 8192 (+35%, Fig. 4a/7a). DD's natural mixture averages **244 ms** — statistically the same as the baseline running at 2k (243 ms). That is the paper's cleanest sentence: *8k context for the price of 2k.* But this only works when attention is a big slice of the pie. My check reproduces their 1B number (attention = 25.0% of FLOPs → predicted +33% latency vs measured +35%) and then says attention is only **5.3%** of FLOPs for a 120B-class model at 8k. *Why it matters for V5:* at 120B/8k the DD speedup is worth ~5%, not 20%. At 32k it is worth ~18%, and at 128k ~47%. Adopt DD for accuracy and stability now; the compute argument only becomes compelling if we push context past 8k.

**5. Benchmarks have a native length, and the model inherits whichever length you trained on.** Training a 1B on a *single* bucket for 17B tokens and sweeping the bucket produces an inverted U for commonsense reasoning, language understanding, and world knowledge — all peaking around length **1024** and *degrading* by 4–5 points by 8192 — while reading comprehension keeps climbing to 4096 (Fig. 5a). The reason is train/test length alignment: those benchmarks' own documents are short (CSR median ≈2⁶ tokens, WK ≈2⁶·⁵) whereas RC sits at ≈2⁹·⁴ and MDQA-10/20/30 at ≈2¹¹/2¹²/2¹²·⁵ (Fig. 5b). *Why it matters for V5:* "long-context lane" and "MMLU-style short-benchmark lane" are in genuine tension, and this paper prices the tension. Training everything long is a measurable regression on the short benchmarks, so the long lane must be a *slice*, never a phase that dominates.

**6. It is really the length, not the content — but content shifts too.** The obvious objection is that long documents are different *stuff*. They tested it: take bucket D13 (8192) and chop each sequence into eight 1024s (`D13→10`), then globally shuffle. Same content, shorter sequences: regular average rises **44.5 → 47.1 (+2.6)**. So length alone is worth 2.6 points. But `D13→10` still trails native `D10` by **0.9** (47.1 vs 48.0) — long documents genuinely are worse-correlated with these benchmarks. And the reverse operation does nothing: concatenating eight random D7 sequences into 1024-token blocks (`D7→10`) scores **40.8**, identical to D7 itself (Fig. 5c). *Why it matters for V5:* you cannot manufacture long-context ability by gluing short documents together — which is exactly what a concat-and-chunk "long-context lane" would be doing. Long-context data must be natively long.

**7. Order the lengths short→long, and cycle it.** Sampling odds per bucket define the curriculum. Growing (short-first) beats uniform; shrinking is a catastrophe. At an *identical* length mixture (2³⁴ tokens from each of D8…D13), Uniform gives MDQA-avg 23.0 and regular 53.8; `Grow-P2` with 8 cycles gives **28.0 / 54.4**; `Shrink-P100` (long-first) collapses to **19.6 / 50.3** (Table 2). The cyclic part matters because a single monotone ramp only shows long sequences at the very end, when LR has already decayed to near-zero — so long-context learning happens when the model can barely move. Eight cycles interleave lengths against the cosine schedule (Fig. 6). *Why it matters for V5:* this is a **+5.0 MDQA-30 gain for zero extra tokens and zero extra compute**, and it directly warns against our instinct to park all the long data in the low-LR anneal.

**8. Short-first is also a stability device.** Long sequences produce extreme gradient variance early in training. Trained at LR = 10⁻² with **gradient clipping disabled**, baseline-8k throws roughly eight loss spikes to 7.5–8.1 between steps 1.6k and 5.5k; DD with `Grow-P100` is smooth and ends lower (~2.6 vs ~3.0, Fig. 8). *Why it matters for V5:* this is independent corroboration of our V4 incident (sudden Hindi increase → ~150× gradient-norm spike, mitigated by blending transitions over billions of tokens). Ramping a *difficulty-correlated* axis instead of step-changing it is the general fix, and length is the cheapest such axis to ramp.

## Method, in order

1. **Tokenize** documents normally (EleutherAI/gpt-neox tokenizer, vocab 50,432).
2. **Decompose per document.** For document `d` of length `l = 2^i₁ + 2^i₂ + … + 2^i_k` (binary expansion), emit `k` *adjacent* sequences of those lengths; assign each to bucket `D_{i_j}`. Every token lands in exactly one bucket; every sequence is a contiguous span of one document. Applied at document level → embarrassingly parallel, negligible overhead, no global sort (contrast ICLM: >1 week on 96 CPUs + 16 GPUs for 400B tokens).
3. **Result of step 2 on RefinedWeb:** token mass peaks at D9 (512) ≈16.7%, then D10 ≈15.5%, D8 ≈10.2%, D11 ≈9.6%, D12 ≈5.9%, D13 ≈3.6%, with a tail out to D17 (Fig. 3b). Most tokens in `Dᵢ` come from documents with `2ⁱ ≤ l < 2^{i+1}`; the remainder are rolled over from longer documents. Capping max context at 8192 folds buckets D14–D17 into D13, which is why the trainable "Natural" mixture reads 3/6/10/17/21/17/13/9 over D6…D13 (Table 1).
4. **Choose a length mixture** `{nᵢ}` = tokens to draw from each bucket (Table 1 enumerates Natural, Equal, 1k-only, ≤2k, ≥256, Mid, ≥1k).
5. **Choose a curriculum** `{oᵢ}` = sampling odds per bucket, and a cycle count `c` (Table 2).
6. **Train with VSL + curriculum (Algorithm 1).** Partition each `Dᵢ` into `c` non-overlapping random subsets of `nᵢ/c` tokens. For each cycle `j`: while any subset is non-empty, set odds to `oᵢ` for non-empty buckets and **0 for empty ones**, normalize to probabilities, sample bucket `i`, then draw `b/2ⁱ` sequences **without replacement** for one optimization step. Repeat until all subsets drained, then advance the cycle.
7. **No document masking needed** — sequences are single-document by construction, so `Dᵢ` steps need no attention mask beyond causal.
8. **Positional encoding:** RoPE, and they explicitly recommend base frequency **100,000 instead of 10,000 from scratch** (Table 4). QuALITY eval bumps θ to 200k at inference with no fine-tuning.
9. **Evaluate** on 14 "regular" benchmarks via LLM Foundry (CSR: PIQA-0, COPA-0, OBQA-10; LU: Lambada-OpenAI, HellaSwag-0, Winograd-3, WinoGrande-5; RC: SQuAD-3, BoolQ-0, CoQA-0; WK: Jeopardy-3, ArcEasy-3, ArcChallenge-3, WikiDataQA-3) plus long-context MDQA-10/20/30, TOEFL, QuALITY. Long-context Avg = mean(MDQA-avg, TOEFL, QuALITY) — verified: DD (27.93+30.7+34.2)/3 = 30.9. ✓

## Assumptions

- **Length is a proxy for difficulty ("short = easy").** → Hides that for us difficulty is *content* (B0 nursery → B5 research), and content difficulty is only loosely length-correlated. A 200-token competition math problem is hard; a 9k-token forum thread is easy. Our difficulty ladder and DD's length ladder are different axes that will fight unless we schedule them jointly.
- **Sequence length is the only thing being varied; the corpus is one homogeneous web pool.** → Hides all lane interaction. In a 7-lane mixture, length correlates with lane (repo-level code, books, agent trajectories are long; QA pairs and Indic snippets are short). So a length curriculum **is** a covert lane curriculum: short-first means web/Indic-first and code/agentic-last. Their own D13-vs-D13→10 gap (0.9 points at matched length) is proof that length and content are entangled even inside one corpus.
- **Tokenizer length ≈ semantic length.** → Breaks hardest for **Indic**. Devanagari and other Indic scripts fragment far worse than English under a mostly-English BPE, so a semantically short Hindi document lands in a *higher* bucket than an equivalent English one. Under `Grow-P2`, higher buckets are sampled late in each cycle ⇒ **the Indic share of tokens is not stationary in time**, and its peak lands where LR is lowest. Given the V4 150× gradient-norm incident on a Hindi step-change, this is a real risk that the paper is completely silent on. Mitigation: enforce the Indic floor *per bucket*, not globally.
- **Every bucket holds enough tokens to be drawn without replacement for a whole cycle.** → Directly breaks the **agentic** lane. Algorithm 1 sets odds to 0 for empty buckets, so a lane with 0.63B unique tokens needing ~63× repetition at 2T empties its high buckets almost immediately and then *silently drops out of the sampler*. DD never tests a bucket 100× smaller than its neighbours. Our protected floor must be implemented as with-replacement resampling inside the bucket, which DD's design explicitly avoids.
- **Each optimization step is length-homogeneous, and that's harmless.** → Hides per-step mixture variance. A step at D13 with `b = 2¹⁹` yields only 64 sequences, so if long documents skew toward one lane, individual steps become lane-skewed. Tolerable at our batch sizes (`b` ≈ 4–16M tokens ⇒ 512–2048 sequences per 8k step) but it means gradient composition is no longer i.i.d. across steps.
- **The measured speedup transfers across scale.** → False in the direction that hurts us, and the paper's own Limitations section concedes it: "gains … are significant only when the target sequence length is long enough." Their 3B outlier (+88%) is a head-dim-80 FlashAttention incompatibility, not a data fact.
- **Longer-than-target extrapolation is out of scope** ("orthogonal … can be applied post-pretraining"). → Nothing here licenses 32k/128k claims. Max context trained is 8192; the longest *real* eval context is ~6k (MDQA-30).
- **Regular-average is a fair scalar.** → It's an unweighted mean of 14 benchmarks, four of which (RC) *want* long sequences and ten of which want short. The composite therefore understates the length tension that Fig. 5a exposes per-category.

## Implementation nits

- **Code:** `github.com/apple/ml-dataset-decomposition` (stated as "to be available"). Training stack: OpenLM (`github.com/mlfoundations/open_lm`), PyTorch, **FSDP + bf16 mixed precision**, **xformers** attention (FlashAttention2 kernel for the timing study). Hardware: nodes of **8×H100 80GB**, 192 CPU cores, 2000 GB RAM, AWS EFA interconnect. Evals via **LLM Foundry v0.7.0**. Best-fit packing baseline: their own best-fit-decreasing implementation (Appendix D); ICLM via `github.com/swj0419/in-context-pretraining`.
- **Baseline hyperparameters (Table 7):** AdamW, β₁ 0.9, β₂ **0.95**, cosine + warmup, **max LR 3e-3**, **cooldown LR 3e-5** (cosine floor = 1% of peak), warmup **5,000** steps, grad-norm clipping **1.0**, global batch **2¹⁹ tokens/step**, weight decay **0.1**, **z-loss 1e-4**. Explicitly *not* tuned for either method — "we always use the same hyperparameters when using either the baseline method or ours."
- **Per-experiment deltas:** §3.3/3.4/3.6 use **20,000** warmup steps and 103B tokens (96×2³⁰). Dataset scaling scales warmup with budget: 5k (2³⁴), 5k (2³⁵), 10k (2³⁶), 20k (2³⁷), 40k (2³⁸). Model scaling: 1B → LR 3e-3, b=2¹⁹, 32 GPUs; 3B → LR 2e-3, b=2²⁰, 64 GPUs; 7B → LR 1e-3, b=2²², 128 GPUs. Alternative dataset: 160M → LR 5e-3, WD 0.033, 16 GPUs; 410M → LR 4e-3, WD 0.066, 16 GPUs; 1B → LR 3e-3, WD 0.1, 32 GPUs.
- **Architectures:** OpenLM-160M (d 768, L 12, H 12, 162,435,840 params); 410M (1024/24/16, 411,665,408); 1B (2048/24/16, 1,439,893,504); 3B (2560/32/**32**, 2,796,096,000 — per-head dim 2560/32 = **80**, *not* FlashAttention-compatible, which is the sole cause of its +88% outlier); 7B (4096/32/32, 6,889,672,704).
- **Sequence length / packing:** max context **8192** everywhere; buckets are powers of two, D6 (64) … D13 (8192) in trainable mixtures, natural decomposition extends to D17 (131,072). Sampling is **without replacement** within per-cycle non-overlapping subsets; empty buckets get odds 0. Tokens-per-step invariant `2ⁱ × b/2ⁱ = b`.
- **Data:** RefinedWeb filtering of Common Crawl, **~525B tokens**, EleutherAI/gpt-neox tokenizer, **vocab 50,432**. Alternative dataset for Table 3 = 137B tokens; §3.5 calls it **DataComp-LM**, but the appendix table caption calls it "an improved refined-web pipeline applied to Common Crawl" (see inconsistencies below).
- **RoPE:** θ = 10,000 for §3.2 and Table 1; **θ = 100,000** for §3.4, §3.5, §3.6 and recommended for from-scratch training; **θ = 200,000 at inference, no fine-tuning**, for QuALITY (whose contexts exceed 8192).
- **Eval nits:** they patched the MDQA scorer from `liu2024lost` to take the first *non-empty* output line rather than the text before the first newline (models often emit leading blank lines) — a scoring change that inflates all MDQA numbers relative to the official script. TOEFL/QuALITY use MMLU-style per-choice mean log-probability and `argmax`. **Note the paper's own §3 text says the choice is "the lowest mean log probability" while Appendix B.3 says `argmax` — the appendix is correct.**
- **Internal inconsistencies found (cite carefully):**
  1. §3.2 says "the number of tokens per optimization step is fixed at **256**, irrespective of sequence length." With 2³⁴ total tokens that implies 67M steps. Appendix B says these runs use Table 7's hyperparameters ⇒ **2¹⁹ tokens/step** (32,768 steps). Treat "256" as garbled; **cite 2¹⁹**.
  2. Table 4 lists DD≥256 at θ=100k with **MDQA 24.9**; Table 2's Uniform row is the same configuration and lists **23.0**. The θ=10k rows agree with Table 1 exactly (53.8/20.1), so the discrepancy is confined to the 100k row — ~1.2× the stated MDQA std.
  3. Fig. 5's caption says "concatenating (**D7→13**)" but the figure and body both say **D7→10**. Body/figure are right.
  4. Table 3 is described as **DataComp-LM** in §3.5 and as an "improved refined-web pipeline" in the appendix caption for the same runs.
- **What is absent:** no MoE, no multilingual anything, no code-specific evaluation, no data filtering or quality selection, no SFT/RLVR, no anneal or cooldown phase (the cosine simply decays to 3e-5), no context beyond 8192, no ablation of the *long-share* between 0% and 9.4%, and no seed replication for Tables 1/2/5.

## Assignment mapping

**1. Budget share for every capability lane (sum 100%).** → **Challenges the long-context share; silent on the rest.** Usable: RefinedWeb's natural decomposition puts **9.4%** of tokens at 8192 and **22.9% at ≥4k** (Table 1, Natural = 3/6/10/17/21/17/13/9 of 96), and raising the 8k share to **25%** moved MDQA-avg only **21.9 → 22.1**. So our 6% long-context lane is *below* what plain web already supplies as a length property; the lane is only additive if it buys *content* web lacks. Silent on code/agentic/reasoning/Indic/STEM shares — DD never varies content at all.

**2. Indic split across verified / unverified / translated / synthetic.** → **Silent, and dangerously so.** The paper has no multilingual experiment. But its mechanism creates an implicit hazard we must answer: bucketing is by *token* length, so tokenizer inflation pushes Indic documents into higher buckets, and `Grow-P2` samples high buckets late in each cycle. Quote that makes it actionable: "at every optimization step, we sample *without replacement* a batch with `b` tokens from bucket `Dᵢ` with probability `pᵢ`. **If a bucket is empty, we exclude it from sampling.**" A per-tier Indic floor must therefore be enforced *inside* each bucket, or tier A (114B real tokens) will drain from D12/D13 first and vanish from the sampler.

**3. Agentic / reasoning / long-context as first-class pretrain slots.** → **Strongly supports long-context; challenges the mechanics for agentic.** Usable: "test accuracy is ≈0 unless pretraining sequence length is greater than the test context length, which is ~2k, 4k, and 6k for MDQA-10, -20, -30." Mixtures lacking D12/D13 score **0.0–0.4** on MDQA-20/30 (Table 1, "≤2k" and "Mid"). Long-context must be a pretrain slot — it cannot be recovered later within this paper's evidence, and it cannot be faked by concatenation (D7→10 = D7 = 40.8). Challenge for agentic: with 0.63B unique tokens needing ~63× repetition, without-replacement per-cycle sampling plus empty-bucket exclusion will starve it; we need with-replacement resampling that DD explicitly does not do.

**4. Protected always-on floor (Indic + agentic + reasoning).** → **Challenges our implementation.** A length curriculum makes every lane's *effective* share non-stationary even when its nominal share is fixed, because lane membership correlates with document length. Quote: "we consider curricula that shift from short to long sequences at different paces." Combined with `Shrink-P100`'s **−3.5 regular / −3.4 MDQA** collapse, this says ordering effects on this axis are large enough (±5 points) to swamp a 2% floor. Our floor must be specified as "8% of accepted tokens **in every bucket and every cycle**," not 8% of the run.

**5. Anneal reserve — which best data is held back for the cooldown.** → **Challenges the reserve, on-topic and with numbers.** This is the sharpest contradiction in the brief. Quote: "if we only see long sequences toward the end of training, long sequence learning occurs only when the learning rate is too small." Their fix is *cyclic* curricula, and it works: `Grow-P2` 1 cycle → 8 cycles takes MDQA-avg **25.9 → 28.0** and CSR **60.9 → 62.8**; `Grow-P100` 1 cycle → 8 cycles takes CSR **60.8 → 63.2** and regular **54.1 → 54.9**. Our plan reserves long CoT and long agent trajectories *for* the anneal — i.e. exactly the monotone single-cycle design this paper measures as inferior on the short-benchmark categories. Recommendation: expose the reserve **cyclically** (e.g. 4–8 passes across the run with the peak in the anneal) rather than holding it entirely for the low-LR tail. Note the counter-evidence too: single-cycle wins RC (47.3 vs 46.3) and Grow-P100×8 *loses* MDQA (27.0 → 24.1), so "cycle everything" is not unconditional.

**6. Each lane tied to the benchmarks it is meant to win.** → **Supports, and supplies the mechanism.** Quote: "the accuracy of commonsense reasoning, language understanding, and world knowledge shows an inverted U-shape behavior with respect to pretraining sequence length, while reading comprehension benefits from longer sequences." Fig. 5b gives each benchmark's native length (CSR ≈2⁶, WK ≈2⁶·⁵, LU ≈2⁷·³, RC ≈2⁹·⁴, MDQA-10/20/30 ≈2¹¹/2¹²/2¹²·⁵). Concretely: our long-context lane funds MDQA-style multi-doc retrieval and TOEFL/QuALITY; it *taxes* MMLU-style short benchmarks by ~4–5 points if it grows past a slice (CSR 60.0 → 55.5 from length 1024 → 8192).

**7. Demand vs unique token supply.** → **Supports with a reframing that helps our 0.1T long-context supply problem.** Long-context "supply" is not a separate corpus — it is a property of corpora we already hold. Quote: "most tokens in a bucket `Dᵢ` are extracted from documents with length `2ⁱ ≤ l < 2^{i+1}` … This demonstrates the efficacy of the method in retaining original document length, **especially for long documents, which are scarce.**" At 2T, the natural 9.4% D13 share of a 4.5T-token web pool is ~423B tokens of genuinely 8k-contiguous text — vastly more than our 0.1T long-context inventory — available at **zero** repetition cost simply by decomposing instead of concat-and-chunking. That converts our long-context lane from a repeat-limited scarcity into a packing decision. Caveat: web 8k spans are not *multi-document reasoning* data, so the curated 0.1T still has a distinct job.

## What not to overclaim

- **"6× faster" is not a number we can put in a 120B README.** It is a 410M compound of ~4× data efficiency × ~1.2–1.3× step-time, measured at 8k on a 1.4B-parameter-class stack. The compute half is a function of the attention FLOPs share, which I computed and validated against their own measurement (predicted 25.0% at d=2048/s=8192 → +33% latency vs their measured +35%; predicted 14.3% at d=4096 vs measured +23%). At **d≈12288 (120B-class), s=8192, attention is only 5.3% of FLOPs**, so DD's wall-clock ceiling there is ~5%, not 20% — the paper concedes this in Limitations ("gains … are significant only when the target sequence length is long enough"). The accuracy gains have no such scale dependence in their data (+3.0/+1.5/+2.7 at 1B/3B/7B) and are the reason to adopt DD. `[INFERENCE]` on the 5.3% figure — it is my arithmetic, calibrated to their measurements, not a number in the paper.
- **The accuracy tables are single-seed and the paper tells us the noise floor is large.** Only §3.2's length-bias runs are seed-averaged (n=2, std ≈0.3 regular / **≈1.6 MDQA**). Tables 1, 2, 3, 5 and Figs. 1, 4b, 8 are one run per cell. So: the Natural-vs-≥1k MDQA difference (21.9 vs 22.1) is **noise and must not be cited as a ranking**; the MDQA-10 plateau across 2k/4k/8k (20.8/21.2/20.4) is flat *within noise* rather than proven flat; and even the flagship +5.0 curriculum gain is a single 3σ-ish observation. The unexplained 23.0-vs-24.9 gap between Table 2 and Table 4 for the same configuration is a live demonstration of that variance.
- **Nothing here is about data quality, and nothing here reaches past 8k.** DD selects, filters, and weights **nothing** — it only re-cuts an already-chosen corpus, and every result is on unfiltered RefinedWeb/DCLM web text with no code, math, multilingual, or synthetic component. So it cannot arbitrate our code/reason/STEM shares, it cannot speak to the Indic tiers or the synthetic caps from the earlier papers, and its longest trained context is 8192 with a longest *real* eval context of ~6k.
