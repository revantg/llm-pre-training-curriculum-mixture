## Teach this first

**There is a closed form, it has one fitted half-life, and that half-life is 15.4 repetitions.** Effective data $D' = U_D + U_D R_D^{*}(1 - e^{-R_D/R_D^{*}})$ with $R_D^{*} = 15.387756$ fit on 182 runs. Two consequences follow immediately and neither is in our spec. First, **repeating is nearly free for the first few epochs**: 4 epochs costs **+0.5% validation loss** at 8.7B params (44B unique vs 178B unique, same 178B tokens processed) and **nothing measurable downstream**. Second, and far more useful to us, **repeating has a hard ceiling**: $D'$ plateaus at $U_D(1 + R_D^{*}) = 16.39 \times U_D$, so *"no matter how many times we repeat the data, we will not get a better loss than could be obtained with a single epoch on $U_D + U_D R_D^{*}$ fresh tokens"* (§3.1). A 0.627B pool can never be worth more than **10.28B effective tokens**. That is an arithmetic bound on our agentic lane, not a ceiling we chose.

**Three thresholds, and the paper does distinguish them.** Figure 1 (left) prints all three as annotations on one axis: *"Up to ≈ 4 epochs repeating is almost as good as new data"* (red line at the 4-epoch tick), *"Rapidly diminishing returns for more repetitions"*, *"At ≈ 40 epochs, repeating is worthless"* (orange line at the 40-epoch tick). **Worthless ≠ harmful.** Worthless is ~16–40 epochs (§6: *"Meaningful gains from repeating data can be made up to around 16 epochs ($R_D^{*}$) beyond which returns diminish extremely fast"*). Harmful is measured separately and is **not in the closed form at all**: §5 says *"our formula … and its predicted isoLoss contours in Figure 3 do not model the possibility that excess epochs or parameters could hurt performance"*, and Appendix F settles on that formulation deliberately. Harm is where the empirical curves live: Figure 4's 44-epoch arms *rise* mid-training (2.8B: bottoms ≈3.03 at 15B tokens, ends 3.42 at 55B), and Figure 10 shows a 146M model on 100M unique tokens bottoming at **59 epochs** and then climbing monotonically to 1740.

**The pool-size objection dies here — and a different objection replaces it.** Two-Phase's 6/8-epoch ceilings came from pools of 161.5B (math) to 6.2T (crawl); we apply them to 0.627B and 5.0B. This paper's repetition grid runs data budgets down to **1.25B, 1.9B and 4B** unique tokens (Fig 4 table), and its allocation grid down to **100M** — the smallest unique pool anywhere in the paper. So 0.627B and 5.0B are *bracketed by measured pools*, and pool size per se is no longer an extrapolation. What *is* still an extrapolation is the **unit of repetition**: §4 states *"Unlike [40], we always repeat the entire available data rather than subsets of it"*, and Appendix Q names repeating fractions as unmodelled future work. Every one of our seven figures is a **lane fraction** inside a corpus we do not repeat. At corpus level V5 has $R_D = 0$: ~6.3T unique available, 2.08T served. That is the honest gap, and it is a sharper gap than the one we thought we had.

**The regime we are actually in has a name and a citation, and it is not this paper.** Related Work: *"Hernandez et al. 2022 study up-sampling of data subsets and find that repeating only 0.1% of training data 100 times significantly degrades performance. In contrast, our work focuses on repeating the entire pre-training corpus for multiple epochs rather than up-sampling parts of it."* Our agentic lane is 0.627B of 2.08T = **0.030%** of the corpus; Indic tier C is 0.24%; the long-CoT pool is 0.22%. **Hernandez et al. 2022 (arXiv:2205.10487) measures our regime and this paper explicitly does not.** That is the highest-value follow-on read in the whole evidence base.

**When unique data runs out, the paper's measured substitute is *adjacent real* text, not synthetic.** Two strategies tested, zero synthetic: fill the missing budget with Python from The Stack, or relax filters. Code filling is flat to **50%** of the budget (4.2B params: 19-task average 22.1±1.7 at 0% code → **23.8±0.5** at 50%), then falls off a cliff (60% 22.8, 70% 22.0, 80% 20.8, 90% 19.3). The compounding rule they print is the single most quotable sentence for our §3: *"first doubling the available data by adding code and then repeating the new dataset for four epochs results in 8× more training tokens that are expected to be just as good as having had 8× more unique data from the start."* But read the small print: the 2× **does not replicate at 2.8B** (C4 average 20.9±0.4 → 19.9 at 50% code; ex-bAbI 22.0±0.5 → 20.1), it **does not replicate on OSCAR at all** (19.4±0.5 → 17.8), and at 4.2B most of the aggregate gain is **bAbI alone** moving 0.0±0.0 → 23.2±1.2 — the ex-bAbI average goes 23.4±1.8 → 23.8±0.5, i.e. flat. The correct claim is *"adjacent real data buys a 2× token pool at no cost, plus one new capability"*, not *"code improves NL accuracy"*.

**Filtering vs repeating is measured, and the answer is asymmetric.** Perplexity-filtering to the cleanest 25% (44B of 178B) then repeating ~1.9 epochs is the **best strategy in Figure 6**: 4.2B average **25.3** against 22.2±1.4 for one epoch on the unfiltered 84B and 22.8±0.5 for repeating a random 21B subset 4×. Deduplication is the **worst**: 21B of 178B repeated 4× scores **20.5**, i.e. −1.7 vs unfiltered and **−2.3 vs repeating a random subset of the same size for the same number of epochs**. Printed rule: *"we recommend reserving filtering for noisy datasets and using both code augmentation and repeating to increase data tokens."* Both filtering results are **single runs with no seeds**, and the perplexity win *"is only effective at 4.2B parameters"* (Appendix O) — flat at 2.8B.

**Loss is the wrong instrument for a mixture change, and they say so in two places.** §7: *"When comparing across data strategies, loss ceases to be a good evaluation metric as the models are trained on different data distributions."* Appendix P: *"validation loss gives an unfair advantage to models trained on a larger fraction of data from the same distribution."* Figure 19 (left) shows code monotonically *raising* C4 validation loss (2.48 at 10% code → 2.81 at 90%) while Figure 6 shows it flat-to-better downstream; Figure 20 ranks perplexity-filtering *worst* on validation loss and Figure 6 ranks it *best* downstream. Any 1B/3B mixture proxy judged on held-out loss would have rejected the two strategies this paper recommends.

## The closed form, written out

### Chinchilla base (their Eq 7, [42] Approach 3)

$$L(N,U) = \frac{A}{N^{\alpha}} + \frac{B}{U^{\beta}} + E$$

### Generalised to repetition (their Eq 14 — the paper's headline law)

$$L(U_N, U_D, R_N, R_D) \;=\; \frac{A}{\left(U_N + U_N R_N^{*}\left(1 - e^{-R_N/R_N^{*}}\right)\right)^{\alpha}} \;+\; \frac{B}{\left(U_D + U_D R_D^{*}\left(1 - e^{-R_D/R_D^{*}}\right)\right)^{\beta}} \;+\; E$$

with the two inner brackets named **effective data** $D'$ (Eq 5 / Eq 13) and **effective parameters** $N'$ (Eq 6).

| Symbol | Meaning | Value / how obtained |
|---|---|---|
| $L$ | loss on a **held-out test set** (not training loss; Appendix H explains why) | 210M tokens of the C4 validation set, computed after training |
| $D$ | total tokens **processed** | up to 900B |
| $D_C$ | the **unique-data budget** — size of the pool available | 100M / 400M / 1.5B (§5); 1.25B…178B (§6, Fig 4 table) |
| $U_D$ | unique tokens used $=\min\{D_C, D\}$ | — |
| $R_D$ | **repetitions**, i.e. **epochs − 1** ($R_D = D/U_D - 1$) | 0 to 1499 |
| $R_D^{*}$ | **half-life of a repeated token** — the point at which repeated tokens have lost $1/e$ of their value | **15.387756** (Appendix A) |
| $N$ | parameters (embeddings included, Eq 23, $V=50257$, $s=2048$) | 7M … 8.7B |
| $U_N$ | parameters that optimally fit $U_D$; $=\min\{N_{opt}(U_D), N\}$ | $U_N = U_D \cdot 0.051$ (Eq 17) |
| $R_N$ | excess-parameter repetitions $=\max\{N/U_N - 1, 0\}$ | — |
| $R_N^{*}$ | half-life of an **excess parameter** | **5.309743** (Appendix A) |
| $A,\alpha$ | parameter-term scale / exponent | $A = e^{6.255414} = \mathbf{521}$, $\alpha = \mathbf{0.3526596}$ |
| $B,\beta$ | data-term scale / exponent | $B = e^{7.3049974} = \mathbf{1488}$, $\beta = \mathbf{0.3526596}$ ($\alpha$ tied to $\beta$) |
| $E$ | irreducible loss | $E = e^{0.6254804} = \mathbf{1.87}$ |

### The fully-substituted form the paper itself prints (Eq 17)

$$L(U_D, R_N, R_D) = \frac{521}{\left(U_N + 5.3\,U_N\left(1 - e^{-R_N/5.3}\right)\right)^{0.35}} + \frac{1488}{\left(U_D + 15.4\,U_D\left(1 - e^{-R_D/15.4}\right)\right)^{0.35}} + 1.87, \quad U_N = U_D \cdot 0.051$$

### Units and fitting range — the part that decides whether we may cite it

- **$R_D^{*}$, $R_N^{*}$ are dimensionless repetition counts.** $R_D^{*} = 15.388$ means 15.4 *repetitions*, i.e. **16.4 epochs**. §6: *"we found $R_D^{*} \approx 15$, corresponding to 15 repetitions (or 16 epochs)."*
- **Fitted on 182 samples**, *"with parameters varying from 7 million up to 9 billion and epochs ranging from 1 to 500"* (Appendix A). LBFGS from a grid $R_N^{*}, R_D^{*} \in \{0, 4, \ldots, 20\}$, Huber + log-sum-exp objective (Eq 16), with $A, B, E, \alpha, \beta$ **frozen** to the Appendix-B C4 values.
- **$A,B,E,\alpha,\beta$ fitted separately on 54 samples** of Chinchilla's own C4 IsoFLOP runs (Appendix B, Eq 19–20), with $\alpha$ tied to $\beta$ because [42] reported $a=b=0.5$ for C4. Sanity check they report: at the Gopher budget $5.76\times10^{23}$ FLOPs the fit gives $N_{opt}=70.0$B / $D_{opt}=1.37$T against Chinchilla's C4 IsoFLOP 73B / 1.3T.
- **Outliers deliberately removed** before fitting: *"We removed outliers referenced in Appendix F from our fitting, as our formulas do not allow for excess parameters or excess epochs to negatively impact performance."* So the law is fit to the *non-harmful* subset by construction.
- **Implied per-repetition decay** $\delta \approx 6\times10^{-2}$ for data (0.19 for parameters) — each pass retains ~94% of the previous pass's value.
- **Fit quality, and the caveat that matters (Table 1, all on the same 182 samples).** Eq 14 (the headline): loss 0.0158, $R^2 = 0.7722$. No decay at all (Chinchilla unmodified): $R^2 = 0.4452$. Decay $D$ only: $R_D^{*} = 2.9157$, $R^2 = 0.7354$. Decay $N$ only: $R_N^{*} = 713.0$, $R^2 = 0.4488$. **The un-approximated geometric form (Eq 10) fits slightly better** — loss 0.0155, $R^2 = 0.7988$ — with $\delta_D = 0.0104$, which implies a plateau at $(1-\delta)/\delta \approx 95\times U_D$ rather than $16.4\times U_D$. Eq 18 agrees ($R_D^{*} = 0.0105$ as a rate ⇒ asymptote $95.7\times$). **So the 16.4× cap is parameterisation-dependent: the better-fitting form puts the asymptote ~6× higher.** The paper chooses Eq 14 *"as it has fewer terms, and we find it easier to interpret"*, and shows the approximation costs only 1.8% in predicted loss at $\delta = 0.25$ (Appendix A worked example: Eq 10 gives $D'=3.05$, Eq 14 gives $3.21$, and $(3.21^{0.353}/3.05^{0.353})-1 = 1.8\%$). Cite 16.4× as the paper's stated cap; **flag that it is the conservative of two fits with near-identical $R^2$.**
- **Excess parameters decay ~3× faster than repeated data** ($R_N^{*}=5.31 < R_D^{*}=15.39$), which is why the data-constrained efficient frontier bends toward *epochs* rather than parameters — the opposite of what Galactica assumed.
- **The alpha-beta variant** (Appendix F Eq 22) *does* let epochs and parameters hurt, and fits 296 samples including the harmful ones: $R_D^{*}=26530.611$, $R_N^{*}=2040.8163$, loss 0.0596, $R^2=0.5110$. The paper rejects it: *"we do not have a rigorous mathematical justification for this alpha-beta decay formulation which could be the cause of the incorrect return predictions."* **There is therefore no fitted law anywhere in the paper for when repetition becomes harmful.**

### The three thresholds, with exact wording and location

| Threshold | Number | Exact wording | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Safe / negligible** | **4 epochs** | *"We find that with constrained data for a fixed compute budget, training with up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible changes to loss compared to having unique data."* | **Abstract** |
| Safe (restated, with downstream) | **4 epochs** | *"differences tend to be insignificant among models trained for up to 4 epochs and do not lead to differences in downstream task performance"* | **§1 Introduction** |
| Safe (quantified) | **+0.5%** | *"the $N=8.7$ billion parameter model trained for four epochs ($D_C=44$ billion unique tokens) finishes training with only 0.5% higher validation loss than the single-epoch model ($D_C=178$ billion unique tokens)"* | **§6** |
| Safe (downstream, quantified) | **4 epochs = 25% budget** | *"For repeating data, differences in downstream performance are insignificant for up to around 4 epochs (25% budget) and then start dropping"* | **§7** |
| Safe (figure annotation) | **≈4 epochs** | *"Up to ≈ 4 epochs repeating is almost as good as new data"* | **Fig 1 left, red annotation** |
| **Diminishing** | **16 epochs** | *"Meaningful gains from repeating data can be made up to around 16 epochs ($R_D^{*}$) beyond which returns diminish extremely fast."* | **§6, final sentence** |
| **Worthless** | **≈40 epochs** | *"At ≈ 40 epochs, repeating is worthless"* | **Fig 1 left, orange annotation** — and note this line sits **beyond the trained dots on that panel**, i.e. in the fitted-extrapolation region |
| Value tends to zero | — | *"with more repetition, the value of adding compute eventually decays to zero"* | **Abstract** |
| Absolute cap | **16.39×** | *"no matter how many times we repeat the data, we will not get a better loss than could be obtained with a single epoch on $U_D + U_D R_D^{*}$ fresh tokens"* | **§3.1** |
| **Actively harmful (loss)** | **44 epochs** | *"Models trained on data that is repeated for multiple epochs have consistently worse loss and **diverge if too many epochs are used**"* — the 44-epoch arms in all three panels rise mid-training | **Fig 4 caption + panels** |
| Actively harmful (loss, small pool) | **>59 epochs** | *"Regardless of deduplication we find 59 epochs to be optimal"*; loss then rises monotonically to 1740 epochs | **Appendix E, Fig 10** |
| Actively harmful (downstream) | **44 epochs** | 19-task average collapses: 20.9±0.4 → **14.1±0.4** (2.8B C4); mean of five repetition rows **22.34 → 15.24** | **Tables 4–9** |
| Actively harmful (mechanism) | — | *"Adding parameters and epochs causes the loss to decrease and eventually increase again, suggesting that too much compute can hurt performance."* | **§5, final paragraph** |

**Verdict on the two thresholds: the paper distinguishes them, but only "worthless" is in the closed form.** "Harmful" appears only as empirical curves and is explicitly excluded from Eq 14. Between them, at 7 and 14 epochs, the cost is real but bounded: mean 19-task average across five rows **−1.00** at 7 epochs and **−2.26** at 14.

### Interactions — does the safe epoch count depend on…

| Interacts with | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Parameter count** | **No, over 2.8B → 8.7B.** The 4-epoch penalty is flat: +0.58% / +0.69% / +0.68% final test loss for 2.8B / 4.2B / 8.7B (Fig 5 left, read at the 25%-unique tick against the 100% dotted reference). Downstream 4-epoch deltas: −0.3 / −1.2 / +0.7 / +0.6 / +0.6 across Tables 4/5/6/8/9. **But** parameter count interacts violently with *pool* size in the other direction: $R_N^{*}=5.31$ means excess parameters decay ~3× faster than repeated tokens, so at fixed FLOPs and constrained data you should **shrink the model and repeat more** (§5, Fig 1 right, Table 8). |
| **(b) Unique-pool size** | **Not in the law — $R_D^{*}$ is a single global constant, not a function of $U_D$.** That is a modelling assumption, never a measurement. Empirically the *loss-minimising* epoch count varies enormously with the pool/compute ratio: **59** epochs at 100M unique (146M params, Fig 3 / Fig 10), **~750** at 400M (220M params, Fig 8 left), **~400** at 1.5B (573M params, Fig 8 right), **9.7** at 25B unique under a $9.3\times10^{21}$ FLOP cap (Table 8), **12.75** at 106B unique under $5.4\times10^{22}$ (Galactica, Appendix G). Those basins are *very* flat — the 3.32-contour in Fig 8 left spans roughly 20 to 700 epochs. |
| **(c) Compute budget** | **Yes, decisively — the 4-epoch claim is explicitly conditional on it.** "for a fixed compute budget" is in the abstract sentence. The safe count is a statement about *relative* loss at matched FLOPs against a run with 4× more unique data; the *optimal* count is a different question whose answer rises with compute at fixed pool (9.7 epochs at $10^{22}$ on 25B unique; 12.75 at $5.4\times10^{22}$ on 106B). |
| **(d) Regularisation / dropout** | **Never varied. Hypothesised only.** All runs use *"a dropout rate of $0.1$, a weight decay rate of $0.1$"* and grad-clip 1.0, fixed (Appendix S). μP was tested and made things **worse** (Fig 11: 6.53 vs 6.09 final test loss at ~200M params on 100M tokens for one epoch), and *"also with μP excessive parameters hurt"*. Appendix F: *"μP only covers hyperparameters such as the learning rate, but not explicit regularization hyperparameters like dropout rates, **which we hypothesize** would prevent this behavior."* Appendix Q: *"The returns from additional epochs may heavily depend on hyperparameters such as learning rate, dropout, or the optimizer choice. It is likely that increasing the learning rate, for example, would lead to diminishing returns from additional epochs kicking in earlier."* **Unmeasured, and flagged by the authors as a first-order sensitivity.** |
| **Metric: loss only, or downstream too?** | **Both, and the downstream half has error bars.** Held-out test loss on 210M C4 validation tokens; and **19 downstream datasets × 0,1,2,3,4,5 shots = 114 scores per model**, rescaled from each task's random baseline to 1, averaged. **Five seeds** for most 2.8B and 4.2B models with std errors reported; the 8.7B rows are single runs with no error bars. Loss is used for §5/§6 (same distribution), downstream for §7 (different distributions), and the paper says why. |

## Method, in order

1. **Fix the architecture and hyperparameters once.** GPT-2 architecture and tokenizer, $V=50257$, seq len 2048, bf16, Adam $\varepsilon=$1e-8, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$ for the two smaller FLOP budgets else 0.999, dropout 0.1, weight decay 0.1, clip 1.0. LR: 1% linear warm-up to **2e-4**, cosine decay to **2e-5** (10× decay over each run, following [42]; *"different schedules led to different estimates in [46]"*). Batch 256 / 512 / 1024 by size. Megatron-DeepSpeed forked for ROCm, ≤256 AMD MI250X on LUMI, ~3M GPU-hours total.
2. **Nest the data budgets (Fig 2).** *"We ensure that runs using less data (more epochs) always use a subset of the data used in runs with more data (fewer epochs)."* Every $k$-epoch run's pool is a strict prefix subset of the $(k-1)$-epoch run's pool. Shuffle after each epoch. **No early stopping** *"to also explore the extent of overfitting when repeating"*.
3. **Fit the single-epoch C4 coefficients first** (Appendix B) on 54 of Chinchilla's C4 points, $\alpha$ tied to $\beta$.
4. **Fixed-unique-data sweep → Allocation (§5).** $D_C \in \{100\text{M}, 400\text{M}, 1.5\text{B}\}$; 93 / 34 / 37 models each, varying params and epochs. Read off the isoLoss basin.
5. **Fixed-FLOPs sweep → Return (§6).** Three budgets ($9.3\times10^{20}$/2.8B/55B, $2.1\times10^{21}$/4.2B/84B, $9.3\times10^{21}$/8.7B/178B), each against **eight** data budgets giving epochs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 14, 44. Repeat on **OSCAR** (Appendix I) and on **The Pile** (Appendix U, "similar trends").
6. **Fit $R_N^{*}, R_D^{*}$ on 182 runs**, outliers where excess params/epochs hurt removed, $A,B,E,\alpha,\beta$ frozen.
7. **Act on the law once, at scale.** At $9.3\times10^{21}$ FLOPs with 25B unique tokens the frontier says 6.3B params / 9.7 epochs instead of Chinchilla's 8.7B / 7.1 epochs. Result: loss **2.359 vs 2.376**, 19-task average **25.9 vs 23.5**. *"Despite having 27% fewer parameters, this model achieves better loss and downstream performance."*
8. **Substitute strategies (§7),** all at 4.2B / 84B total: Python from The Stack at 10 mixing rates 0–90%; perplexity-filter (KenLM 5-gram on Wikipedia intros, top 25% / 50% / 25-75%); 100-char-span suffix-array deduplication; the ROOTS filter chain. Filtering starts from **178B** (twice the budget) *"as it is easier to gather noisy data and filter it than it is to gather clean data"*.
9. **Evaluate substitutes downstream, not on loss,** for the reason quoted above.

- **$R_D^{*} = 15.387756$, $R_N^{*} = 5.309743$**; 182 samples, 7M–9B params, 1–500 epochs; $\delta_D \approx 6\times10^{-2}$, $\delta_N = 0.19$ · Appendix A.
- **$A=521$, $B=1488$, $E=1.87$, $\alpha=\beta=0.3526596$**; 54 samples · Appendix B, Eq 20. **$U_N = U_D \cdot 0.051$** · Eq 17.
- **Effective-data cap $= 16.39\times$ unique**, reached asymptotically · §3.1. Alternative fit (Eq 10, $R^2$ 0.7988 vs 0.7722) implies **~95×** · Table 1.
- Fit comparison $R^2$: no decay **0.4452**, decay-$N$-only 0.4488, decay-$D$-only 0.7354 ($R_D^{*}=2.92$), **Eq 14 0.7722**, Eq 10 0.7988, alpha-beta 0.5110 · Table 1.

### 1 · Agentic organic ceiling — **4 epochs on 0.627B unique**

**CONFIRMS the number. BREAKS the way we justify the lane.**

- **The 4 epochs is now measured, not borrowed.** It is this paper's headline, stated in the Abstract, restated in §1, quantified at +0.5% loss in §6 and as "insignificant" downstream in §7, and annotated on Fig 1. Across the five repetition rows, 4 epochs scores **+0.08** on the 19-task mean versus 1 epoch, and is nominally *better* in 3 of 5 rows. Two-Phase's Figure 4 gave one MMLU curve at 8B with no error bars; this gives five rows, three of them with 5-seed std errors, on two corpora, plus loss.
- **Does the evidence reach a 0.627B pool?** On pool size, **yes** — 0.627B lies between the 100M allocation grid and the 1.25B repetition grid, and Fig 3's 100M experiment is 93 trained models. This is a genuine upgrade over the borrowed ceiling, whose smallest pool was 161.5B.
- **On the mechanism, no — and this is the finding.** At 4 epochs our agentic lane is 0.627B of 2.08T = **0.030% of the corpus**, repeated while 99.97% of the corpus is not repeated. §4: *"we always repeat the entire available data rather than subsets of it."* Related Work: *"repeating only 0.1% of training data 100 times significantly degrades performance."* Our fraction is *smaller* than Hernandez's 0.1%; our multiplier at 4 epochs is far smaller than 100×. So we sit off both papers' measured grids, closer to this one on multiplier and closer to Hernandez on fraction. **Applying "4 epochs is free" to a 0.030% lane fraction is an extrapolation on the axis this paper names as its own limitation.** Say that plainly rather than claiming coverage.
- **Effective tokens:** 4 epochs of 0.627B = 2.508B nominal → **2.336B effective** (93.1%). §3 books this as "2.508B = 0.125% of 2T"; on the 2.080T whole-plan base the nominal share is **0.121%** and the *effective* share **0.112%**.
- **The gift: a hard cap.** $D' \le 0.627 \times 16.388 =$ **10.28B effective tokens, for any number of epochs, forever.** That is **25.7%** of the 40.0B main-run lane and **22.3%** of the 46.0B whole-plan exposure. Our booked multipliers already sit essentially at the cap and are therefore mostly waste: 63.8× yields 16.13× effective = 10.11B, i.e. **25.3% of the 40.0B it is nominally paying for**; 73.4× yields 16.25× = 10.19B, **22.1% of 46.0B**. Reaching the cap needs ~80 epochs, and the paper measured a **−7.10-point** five-row downstream collapse at 44 (2.8B C4: 20.9±0.4 → 14.1±0.4). **So the reachable-and-safe ceiling on organic agentic supply is 2.34B effective at 4 epochs, or 6.64B at 16 epochs at a measured 1.5–3.8 point downstream cost.** That converts §3's "the generation programme is a choice we defend" into "the generation programme is the arithmetic residual" — a stronger position, because ~74% of the lane is *provably* not fillable with the organic pool at any epoch count.

### Which paper governs which question — the reconciliation that matters

Written after the spec's §2 was rewritten under me (tier C 8% → **4% / 2.53×**) on the strength of `emnlp`'s NVIDIA cap sweep. That rewrite is **correct**, and getting to why it is correct is the most important thing in this brief, because it decides how far any of my seven verdicts may be pushed.

There are two distinct questions and this paper answers only one of them.

| Question | Authority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **How much is a repeated token worth?** | **This paper.** | The fitted law is regime-independent arithmetic: given a repeat factor $m$, $D'/U = 1 + R_D^{*}(1-e^{-(m-1)/R_D^{*}})$ holds whatever the token is. It yields the effective-token discounts (6.9% at 4 epochs, 2.9% at 2.53×, 1.9% at tier B, 0.3% on code) and the **16.39× cap**. Nothing else we hold can produce these. |
| **How many times may I repeat *this source* inside a fixed mixture?** | **NVIDIA (`emnlp`), then Hernandez 2205.10487.** Not this paper. | Per-source upsampling is the regime §4 excludes by construction — *"we always repeat the entire available data rather than subsets of it"* — and Appendix Q defers to future work. NVIDIA measured it directly (8B/300B, cap 1 vs 2 vs 4, sources 18.7B down to 1.5B) and found **cap 1 beats 2 and 4**. Hernandez is the same regime, more extreme: 0.1% repeated 100× *"significantly degrades performance"*. |

**Every one of our seven figures is a question of the second kind.** V5 never repeats its corpus — ~6.3T unique available, 2.08T served, so corpus-level $R_D = 0$. What we call a "repeat factor" is always one lane or tier upsampled against the rest. So my per-row CONFIRMS verdicts should be read as **"the factor is cheap"**, never as **"the factor is permitted"**. The permission question belongs to NVIDIA.

**This resolves what looks like a head-on contradiction.** This paper says 4 epochs of everything is free; NVIDIA says a cap of 1 beats 2 and 4. Both are right, because they are not the same intervention: repeating a whole corpus uniformly changes no source's *relative* weight, while capping per-source changes the mixture itself — and NVIDIA's own Appendix E concedes the confound, since raising the cap also flattens toward uniform. The honest joint statement is: **uniform repetition of everything is nearly free to 4 epochs; upsampling a small source against a large corpus has never been measured to win, at any factor above 1, anywhere in our evidence base.**

**Consequence for the agentic lane, which is the one place this still bites in our favour.** The 16.39× cap is a statement about *value*, not permission, so it survives the regime split intact — and it is the tighter of the two constraints anyway. 0.627B can never be worth more than 10.28B effective tokens even if per-source repetition were free, which it is not. Both papers therefore point the same way on agentic: the lane cannot be filled with real tokens.

> Every repeat factor at or below 5.06× is measured rather than borrowed: **Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models (arXiv 2305.16264v5, NeurIPS 2023)** fits $D' = U_D + U_D R_D^{*}(1 - e^{-R_D/R_D^{*}})$ with **$R_D^{*} = 15.387756$** on 182 runs spanning 7M–9B parameters and 1–500 epochs (Appendix A), and finds **"training with up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible changes to loss compared to having unique data"** (Abstract) — quantified as **+0.5% validation loss at 8.7B params on 44B unique tokens against 178B unique** (§6), with downstream differences insignificant to 4 epochs across 19 tasks, 0–5 shots and five seeds (§7, Tables 4–9). Their repetition grid runs unique pools down to **1.25B tokens** and their allocation grid to **100M**, so our 5.0B and 0.627B pools are bracketed by measured budgets rather than extrapolated from an 8B/1T English run — but they repeat the **entire** corpus (§4: *"we always repeat the entire available data rather than subsets of it"*), whereas every factor below is a lane fraction of a corpus we never exhaust, the one regime their Appendix Q leaves to future work.

**Keep Two-Phase for exactly one claim: domain heterogeneity.** 2305.16264 never splits repetition by domain — every repetition run is one homogeneous web corpus, and $R_D^{*}$ is a single global constant. Two-Phase's **6 epochs for high-quality crawl vs 8 for math** remains the only differentiated per-domain epoch evidence in the entire evidence base. Use it for the *shape* claim (curated/math-like text tolerates more passes than crawl) and 2305.16264 for the *level*. Note the two disagree in direction of conservatism: Two-Phase's math peak at 8 epochs is *above* this paper's 4-epoch safe point and *below* its 16-epoch $R_D^{*}$, so they are compatible.

**For the agentic row, do not replace the ceiling — replace the whole argument.** No paper measures 63.8×. But this one supplies a bound that no ceiling can: effective tokens plateau at $16.39\times U_D$, so **0.627B can never exceed 10.28B effective tokens**, and reaching even that needs ~80 epochs against a measured 6.8-point downstream collapse at 44. A cap is strictly better than a ceiling here: a ceiling says "you are past what was tested", a cap says "the tokens you are claiming do not exist".

## Substitute data — the answer to weakness #1

**What is measured.** Two substitutes, both **real text**, zero synthetic:

1. **Fill the missing budget with an adjacent real corpus.** Up to **50% of a data-constrained budget replaced by Python** from The Stack: 4.2B/84B, 19-task average **22.1±1.7 → 23.8±0.5**, *"no deterioration"*, cliff above 50% (60% 22.8 → 90% 19.3). Headline: **2× effective tokens**. Compounding: **"2× from code, then 4 epochs = 8× training tokens as good as 8× unique from the start."**
2. **Stop filtering, or filter for signal not duplicates.** Perplexity-filter to the cleanest 25% then repeat ~1.9 epochs is the **best strategy tested** (4.2B: 25.3 vs 22.2±1.4 unfiltered, vs 22.8±0.5 for repeating). Deduplicate-then-repeat is the **worst** (20.5). Rule: *"reserving filtering for noisy datasets."*

**What is NOT measured, and we must not imply it is.** No synthetic data of any kind — no rephrasing, no invented textbooks, no distillation, no self-generation, no trajectories, no verifier filtering. The word "synthetic" does not describe any arm of this paper. **On our generation programme it is SILENT.** Our synthetic ceilings still come from `synthetic` 2510.01631 and `collapse` 2404.05090 and nothing here changes them.

**Three honesty conditions on the 2× that a reviewer will find if we do not state them.**
- **It does not replicate at 2.8B.** C4 average 20.9±0.4 → 19.9 at 50% code; ex-bAbI 22.0±0.5 → 20.1. The 2× is positive only at the larger of the two sizes tested, and there is no 8.7B code arm.
- **It does not replicate on OSCAR** (19.4±0.5 → 17.8), because OSCAR already contains code while C4 explicitly removed it. **The substitute only pays when the base corpus genuinely lacks that content.** For us that is a *good* condition — our agentic lane genuinely lacks trajectory-shaped tokens — but it means the gain is a coverage effect, not a code-is-magic effect.
- **Most of the aggregate gain is bAbI.** Ex-bAbI, 0% → 50% is 23.4±1.8 → 23.8±0.5 = **flat**. bAbI alone goes 0.0±0.0 → 23.2±1.2. So the honest claim is: *filling half the budget with adjacent real data costs nothing on the original tasks and buys one new capability that was at zero.* That is precisely the shape of the agentic claim we want to make — which is why we must state it in exactly those terms and not as "+1.7 points".

**What this does to the agentic 2.00%.** Under this paper alone, with **no** synthetic allowance:

| Composition | Nominal lane | Effective tokens | **Share of 2.080T** (nominal) | Measured cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic 0.627B at 4 epochs — the safe point | 2.508B | 2.336B | **0.121%** | none (five-row mean **+0.08**) |
| …plus a licensed 50% adjacent-real fill | 5.016B | 4.672B | **0.241%** | none (4.2B, 50% code: 22.1±1.7 → 23.8±0.5) |
| Organic at 16 epochs ($R_D^{*}$, "meaningful gains" end here) | 10.032B | 6.635B | **0.482%** | **−1.5 to −3.8 pts** (14-epoch column, Tables 4–9) |
| …plus the 50% fill | 20.064B | 13.270B | **0.965%** | stacks both concessions |
| Organic repeated to the 16.39× cap (~80 epochs) | 50.16B | 10.22B | 2.412% | **unreachable** — 44 epochs measured **−7.10 pts** |

Read as a band on the *nominal* share, which is what a mixture percentage means:

- **Measured-free band: 0.121% → 0.241%.** Both legs are settings where this paper measured no cost — 4 epochs of organic supply, optionally doubled by an adjacent-real fill at the 50% licence. **2.00% is 8.3× outside the top of it and 16.6× outside the bottom.**
- **Outer bound, accepting measured costs: 0.482% → 0.965%.** Pushing organic repetition to $R_D^{*}$ = 16 epochs buys 4× the nominal volume for 6.635B effective tokens at a 1.5–3.8 point downstream cost; stacking the fill on top reaches 0.965%. **Even here 2.00% is 2.07× outside.**

Our current §3 says "the defensible band is 0.125%–0.376%. We ship 2.00%, which is 5–16× outside it", built from Collapse's 80/20 and 50/50 arms and Demystifying's ≤30% rephrase allowance. **This paper replaces those borrowed allowances with one fitted law plus one measured substitute, and the answer barely moves: 0.121%–0.241% measured-free, 0.965% at the absolute outer edge.** The floor hardens (0.121% is now a measured point, not an inference) and the ceiling rises only if we accept a stated downstream cost.

**Does it strengthen or weaken the agentic 2.00% defence? Both, and the direction matters for E9.**
- **Strengthens the argument.** It turns "63.8× is outside anything measured" into "repetition can supply at most 10.28B of 40B by arithmetic, so 74% of this lane is necessarily new tokens" — a derivation, not an apology. And it supplies a **measured, non-synthetic fallback** we do not currently offer: fill up to 50% of the lane with adjacent real data (the 362B code pool, the 267M format pool) rather than 91.4% generation. That fallback is better than §3's current FIM fallback, which the spec itself concedes *"creates zero new tokens"*.
- **Weakens the number.** 91.4% generated against a measured 50% adjacent-real licence is the same over-reach shape E9 exists to punish, now with a *measured* band to be outside of rather than a borrowed one. The miss becomes **8.3× against the measured-free ceiling (0.241%)** and **2.07× even against the cost-accepting outer bound (0.965%)** — and note what the fill does *not* buy: filling half the agentic lane with Python supplies **volume and state-tracking**, not tool use, exactly as §3's FIM fallback supplies "tool-call *format* rather than multi-step agency".
- **Net: adopt it, and say so.** §3 already declares non-compliance in its own heading (*"the one non-compliant number"*) and prints the multiple. Citing this paper while keeping 2.00% is only safe *because* we declare the gap. **Citing it as support for 2.00% would be a reversed citation and FATAL.** The right move is to add the cap and the substitute band and let the multiple get worse on the page.

## Assumptions

- **Repetition means repeating the whole corpus.** §4, explicitly, and contrasted with [40] Hernandez et al. → **every V5 figure is a lane fraction. This is the one assumption that breaks on contact with our spec**, and Appendix Q concedes the gap: *"one may need to adapt our scaling laws with an additional parameter to account for the fraction that is repeated and possibly a parameter that captures at what point in training the data is repeated."*
- **A single global $R_D^{*}$, independent of domain, language, pool size and position in training.** → No per-lane epoch ceiling can be derived from this paper. Two-Phase's crawl-6 / math-8 split has no counterpart here.
- **The corpus is homogeneous, and small pools are random slices of it (Fig 2).** → Their 1.25B pool has C4's composition; our 0.627B agentic pool is a single narrow domain. Their design cannot see the difference.
- **Loss can only plateau, never rise, in $R_D$ and $R_N$** — enforced by dropping the harmful runs from the fit. → The law is unusable for "when does repetition hurt", and the paper says so.
- **Hyperparameters are fixed at one commonly-used point** (LR 2e-4→2e-5 cosine, dropout 0.1, WD 0.1, no early stopping). → Appendix Q flags LR/dropout/optimizer as first-order sensitivities on the safe epoch count. **Our anneal repeats scarce data at a decayed LR, which is off this grid in the direction the paper says matters.**
- **Repeating late is worse than repeating early** — *"Repeating earlier in training when most model weights are still randomly initialized is likely to cause less damage than later in training"* (Appendix Q), hedged as "likely", untested. → **Directly challenges §4's reserve design**: we hold the scarcest data (long trajectories at 3.89 epochs of a 360M pool, 3.0B of a 4.6B long-CoT pool) for the very end, and B5 is reserve-only. This paper's speculation points the other way.
- **English web text, GPT-2 architecture, 2048 sequence length, ≤8.7B parameters.** → Silent on Indic, on 120B, on long context, on any mixture, on any curriculum, on any anneal.
- **Downstream = 19 zero-to-five-shot NLU/generation tasks normalised to random baseline.** → No MMLU, no GSM8K, no code benchmark, no agentic or long-context eval. A capability we care about could move without appearing here.
- **The substitute corpus is real text from another domain.** → No synthetic arm of any kind exists.

## Follow-on papers

- **Hernandez et al. 2022, "Scaling Laws and Interpretability of Learning from Repeated Data", arXiv:2205.10487** — **the single highest-value next read in the whole evidence base.** It measures *fraction* repetition, which is what all seven of our figures actually are, and the one number quoted from it (repeating 0.1% of data 100× significantly degrades performance) is directly adverse to our scarce lanes. Would move **agentic (0.030% of corpus), Indic tier C (0.24%), the ULTRA band and the anneal's long-trajectory reserve** simultaneously. If we read one more paper, this is it.
- **Villalobos et al. 2022, "Will we run out of data?", arXiv:2211.04325** — the supply-exhaustion estimates behind this paper's framing. Would let §8 price "acquire more unique tokens" against "repeat what we have" instead of asserting a cleaning priority order.
- **Lee et al. 2021, "Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better", arXiv:2107.06499** — the paper whose perplexity gains this work reproduces and whose *downstream* benefit it fails to find. Needed to resolve §1's 362B effective code pool, which is a dedup-then-repeat decision this paper measured at −2.3 points on web text.
- **Hoffmann et al. 2022, Chinchilla, arXiv:2203.15556** — source of the C4 IsoFLOP points refit in Appendix B. Its C4 coefficients, not its MassiveWeb ones, are what give 120B ⇔ 2.353T; worth confirming before we restate our 0.83×.
- **Kaplan et al. 2020, arXiv:2001.08361** — cited for the $6ND$ cost model *and* as the source of divergent estimates caused by LR schedules and early stopping. Relevant to §7's cost basis, which also uses $6ND$.
- **Kocetkov et al. 2022, The Stack, arXiv:2211.15533** — the Python corpus used as the substitute. Relevant if we adopt a code-fill arm for the agentic lane.
- **Taylor et al. 2022, Galactica, arXiv:2211.09085** — the only production LLM to have deliberately repeated (4.25 epochs on 106B unique), and the case study in Appendix G. The nearest production precedent for a deliberately-repeated pretrain.
- **Yang et al. 2021, μP** (NeurIPS 34:17084–17097; no arXiv id in this bibliography) — Appendix F's negative result. Matters only if we plan to transfer hyperparameters from our 1B/3B probes to 120B, which §7 currently does not address.

## VERDICT

**(a) In scope? Emphatically yes — this is the most in-scope paper in the evidence base for the single question the spec rests on.** It is the only source that (i) fits a closed form for the value of a repeated token, (ii) measures repetition on unique pools smaller than 5B, (iii) reports both loss and downstream accuracy with error bars, (iv) replicates on a second corpus, and (v) measures a substitute for missing data. It replaces a borrowed pair of integers with a fitted constant, and it supplies one number — the 16.39× effective-token cap — that no other paper we hold can produce. **Keep. **B — §3, immediately before the four-row band table:**

> The same law caps what repetition can ever buy: effective tokens plateau at **U·(1 + R\*) = 16.39× unique**, so the 0.627B organic pool can never be worth more than **10.28B effective tokens** at any epoch count — **25.7% of the 40.0B lane**, which makes the generation programme the arithmetic residual rather than a preference. ## What not to overclaim

- **Do not say this paper licenses 5.06× on a 5.0B Indic pool.** It licenses 5 epochs on a 5B *random slice of English web*, with one of five rows showing a real −1.2-point cost, and it never trains on any Indic language. Punjabi appears once, in footnote 2, as motivation.
- **Do not quote "59 epochs is optimal" or "750 epochs is optimal" as a recommendation.** They are argmins inside very flat basins at 100M–1.5B unique tokens under unbounded compute, non-monotone in pool size, and Figure 10 shows loss rising past 59. The paper makes no epoch recommendation beyond "4 is free, 16 is the half-life, 40 is worthless".
- **Do not use the 16.39× cap as permission to repeat more.** It is an upper bound on *value*, not on *safety*: reaching it needs ~80 epochs, and 44 epochs measured a 6.8-point downstream collapse. The reachable-and-safe figure is 4 epochs (93.1% efficient) or 16 epochs at a measured 1.5–3.8 point cost.
- **Do not quote the 16.39× cap as though it were unique.** Table 1's better-fitting Eq-10 form implies ~95×, with $R^2$ 0.7988 against 0.7722. Cite 16.39× as the paper's stated cap *and* say it is the conservative of two near-equivalent fits.
- **Do not claim "code gives +1.7 points".** Ex-bAbI the 4.2B 0%→50% change is 23.4±1.8 → 23.8±0.5, i.e. flat; it does not replicate at 2.8B or on OSCAR; and there is no 8.7B code arm. The defensible claim is *no cost on the original tasks, plus one capability that was at zero*.
- **Do not read the code result as a licence for synthetic data.** There is no synthetic arm. The substitute is real Python from The Stack.
- **Do not cite this paper for a per-lane or per-domain epoch ceiling.** $R_D^{*}$ is one global constant fit to one homogeneous corpus. Two-Phase's crawl-6 / math-8 split remains our only differentiated evidence.
- **Do not treat the 4-epoch result as position-independent.** Appendix Q expects repeating late to hurt more than repeating early, and our anneal repeats the scarcest pools last.
- **Do not judge any V5 mixture proxy on validation loss.** §7 and Appendix P both say loss is distribution-relative; Figures 19 and 20 rank the two winning strategies *last* on loss.
- **Do not present the 6.3B-vs-8.7B downstream gain (+2.4) as robust.** The 8.7B rows are single-seed and the 7-epoch point (23.5) sits anomalously below its 5-epoch neighbour (26.1). The loss version — 2.359 vs 2.376 at −27% parameters — is the defensible form.
- **Do not claim the paper reaches 120B parameters or 2T tokens.** Largest model trained is 8.7B; longest run is 900B tokens; largest unique pool is 178B.
