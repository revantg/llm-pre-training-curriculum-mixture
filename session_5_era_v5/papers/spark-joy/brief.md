## Verdict for our assignment

**High value, and it is the single most directly on-point paper in this batch for the anneal-length question — but it does not say what we want it to say.** It is the paper that coined the end-of-training upsampling probe (Llama 3 and OLMo 2 both cite it by name), it publishes a real phase-length sweep with a full benchmark table at every point, and it publishes the exact before/after mixture weights with token counts. All of that is citable.

**But its sweep bottoms out at 5% of the run (50B tokens), and its headline recommendation is 10–20% of the run.** At our 2T budget the fraction reading of that recommendation is 200–400B tokens, which is 5–10x our ~2% (40B) anneal. So *this paper alone does not defend a 2% anneal, and it only barely reaches a 4% one.* The defense has to be made on **absolute tokens**, not fraction of run: the paper's knee is at 50–100B absolute tokens, our 4% is 80B, and that sits inside their measured band. Our 2% is 40B, which is below every single point they ran.

Two further things it gives us that are worth more than the headline. First, the **sans-math ablation is the best protected-floor evidence in the whole reading list**: removing one domain from the end-of-training mix drove MMLU to **29.71**, i.e. **5.98 pp *below* the model that got no intervention at all (35.69)** — a 100B-token final phase can actively *unlearn* a capability you stop feeding. Second, it is a **pure data intervention with no learning-rate change**, so its +3.95 pp Gauntlet gain is attributable to the mixture alone, separately from whatever an LR decay leg buys (see "What not to overclaim" — treat the two as overlapping, not additive).

Where it is silent it is *totally* silent: no multilingual data, no Indic, no agentic, no long-context (4096 ctx, ALiBi), no synthetic data, no loss curves, no error bars, and it never names a single concrete dataset.

## Teach this first

**1. The problem is that data experiments only become legible at a scale you cannot afford to repeat.** The interesting benchmarks — MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval — are *emergent*: a small model scores at the random-guess floor on them, so any difference you measure between two data mixes at small scale is noise, not signal. ("Emergent" here just means the score stays flat at chance until some FLOP threshold, then climbs.) So the honest way to compare two mixes is two full-scale runs, and nobody has budget for the dozens of runs that a real mixture search needs. *Why it matters for V5:* this is exactly our position. We cannot run 2T twice to test whether Indic should be 12% or 16%, and a 62M proxy (CLIMB's answer) will not register anything on the Indic benchmarks we actually care about.

**2. Their answer: change the mixture only at the very end, and reuse the same expensive prefix for every experiment.** Train once to, say, 0.9T tokens. Then branch: run arm A for the last 0.1T with mixture X, arm B for the last 0.1T with mixture Y. Both arms inherit 0.9T of shared compute, so each *additional* arm costs 10% of a run, not 100%. That is the "order of magnitude cheaper" claim, and it is arithmetic, not a result. *Why it matters for V5:* at 2T/120B a full run is ~1.44e24 FLOPs; a 2%-of-run (40B) probe is ~2.9e22, about **1/50 of a run**. That is our budget line for testing Indic tier weights or the agentic reserve — we can afford maybe a dozen probes, not a dozen runs.

**3. The intervention itself is embarrassingly simple: delete the biggest, dirtiest bucket and scale up everything else.** Their corpus has four buckets. Large-Scale Common Crawl (bulk web, lightly filtered) goes from **34.35% → 0%**. Small-Scale Common Crawl (heavily filtered web) goes **36.70% → 30%**, i.e. essentially untouched. Domain Specific (Wikipedia-class curated sources, includes their math data) goes **7.17% → 35%**, a **4.9x** upsample. Code goes **21.78% → 35%**, a **1.6x** upsample. Note what they did *not* do: they did not cut their filtered web. They say why, in one sentence: "We also maintain Small-Scale Common Crawl at high percentage to prevent a large distribution shift in our pretraining data." *Why it matters for V5:* our anneal preset puts **web at 8%**, down from 34%. Their anneal keeps filtered web at 30%. If a reviewer asks why we cut 4x harder than the paper that invented the technique, we need an answer, and "we split web into raw vs filtered and only zeroed the raw half" is the only good one.

**4. It works, and it works on the benchmarks we are graded on.** Same 7B model, same 1T token budget, same FLOPs — the only difference is the mixture over the last 200B tokens. MMLU 35.69 → **42.59** (+6.90). GSM8K 14.71 → **22.97** (+8.26). HumanEval 17.23 → **23.40** (+6.17). Aggregate Gauntlet Core Average 35.37 → **39.32** (+3.95). For reference, Llama-2 7B trained for **twice** as many tokens scores MMLU 45.51 / GSM8K 14.25 / HumanEval 13.55. So the intervention is worth roughly a doubling of the token budget on the generative benchmarks, and closes most (not all) of the MMLU gap. *Why it matters for V5:* this is the argument for having an anneal leg at all, in a currency our reviewer accepts. It is free — no extra FLOPs — because the leg is carved out of the existing budget, not added to it.

**5. Longer is not better, and the two families of benchmark disagree about where to stop.** They ran the last 5% / 10% / 20% / 30% (= 50B / 100B / 200B / 300B tokens) with the same upsampled mixture. Math and code climb monotonically all the way to 30% (GSM8K 14.71 → 16.98 → 20.47 → 22.97 → **24.56**; HumanEval → **24.17**). Knowledge and comprehension peak early and then fall back: MMLU peaks at **10%** (43.19) and is down to 41.78 by 30%; Gauntlet Core Average peaks at **20%** (39.32) and falls to 38.89; World Knowledge peaks at 10%; Reading Comprehension peaks at 20% (45.45) then drops 2.95 pp. One category, **Language Understanding, is negative at every single length** (61.52 → 61.05 → 60.41 → 60.08 → 60.35). Their conclusion, in their words: "as we increase the fraction of training with domain upsampling beyond 20%, improvements on math and coding benchmarks come at the cost of performance on general language modeling abilities." *Why it matters for V5:* this is the shape of the curve we are betting on. It is steeply concave — the 0→5% step is by far the biggest — which is the honest reason a short anneal can be defensible. But "beyond 20% it hurts" is not "2% is enough," and we must not conflate the two.

**6. The most important number in the paper is one they buried in a side experiment.** They reran the 10% arm with the **math subsets deleted** from the upsampled mixture, keeping the 30/35/35 proportions. The result is not "smaller gain." It is *below the no-intervention baseline on almost everything*: MMLU 43.19 → **29.71** (baseline 35.69), Reading Comprehension 43.35 → **26.48** (baseline 37.02), Commonsense 42.33 → **31.76** (baseline 38.38), Core Average 38.46 → **32.54** (baseline 35.37). Only Programming survives (21.15, actually the best of the three). In 100B tokens — 10% of the run — the model *lost* capability it already had, because the final phase heavily reweighted toward data that does not carry it. *Why it matters for V5:* this is the empirical case for item #4, the protected always-on floor. It says the floor is not just a pretraining property — **the anneal mixture needs its own floor**, because the anneal is where a starved lane gets actively overwritten. Our anneal preset gives web 8% and STEM 10%; this experiment is what happens when a lane goes to 0.

**7. Crucially, they did not touch the learning rate.** The paper's whole schedule is one inverse-square-root decay (peak LR 1.2e-4, LionW optimizer) and there is not one word about changing it at the 0.8T seam. Under a pure inverse-sqrt law the LR at 1.0T is `sqrt(0.8) ≈ 0.894` of the LR at 0.8T — the final leg runs at ~90–100% of full pretraining LR. So **this is not an anneal in the "low LR cooldown" sense at all.** It is a mixture swap at cruising LR. *Why it matters for V5:* our lifecycle defines the anneal as "same CE, curated reserve, **low LR**." Those are two interventions bundled together. Spark-joy isolates the data half and shows it is worth +3.95 Gauntlet on its own. OLMo 2 later isolated the LR half and found the LR decay alone is worth +4.4 OLMES. We should expect our anneal to get both, and we should not attribute all of the gain to either.

**8. They set their base mixture weights by choosing target epoch counts, then backfilling with web.** Not by a search, not by DoReMi — by deciding "small-scale CC and domain-specific data should each be seen 0.5 times, code 1.0 times, and Large-Scale CC fills whatever is left." That single sentence implies the size of every pool: 367.0B at 0.5 epochs means a 734B pool, 71.7B at 0.5 epochs means a 143.4B pool, 217.8B at 1 epoch means a 217.8B pool. *Why it matters for V5:* this is a supply-first mixture rule and it is exactly the discipline item #7 asks for. Applied to us it is brutal about the agentic lane: 8% of an 80B anneal is 6.4B agentic tokens against 0.63B unique = **~10 epochs inside the anneal leg alone**. Spark-joy's reserve never exceeded ~1.1 epochs even in its best arm. An 8% agentic anneal slot is not a reserve, it is a 10x repeat, and we have to say so out loud.

## Method, in order

1. **Build a 4-bucket base corpus** from public datasets, grouped by *character* rather than by source: Large-Scale CC (bulk, lightly filtered), Small-Scale CC (aggressively filtered, smaller), Domain Specific (curated single sources, "e.g. Wikipedia"; includes unnamed math subsets), Code (multi-language). No dataset is ever named beyond "Wikipedia."
2. **Set base weights by target epoch count, not by search.** Pick 0.5 epochs for Small-Scale CC, 0.5 for Domain Specific, 1.0 for Code; fill the remaining budget with Large-Scale CC. Yields 34.35 / 36.70 / 7.17 / 21.78 % (Table 2). The 21.78% code figure is separately justified: "initial experiments indicated that a high percentage of code around 20% boosted programming and reasoning ability without negatively impacting language abilities."
3. **Validate the baseline against a public scaling line.** Evaluate Llama-2 7B/13B/70B on MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval and Gauntlet Core Average; regress log(error) on log(FLOPs); confirm your own 0.5T and 1T checkpoints sit on or below that line (Figure 1). This is the "our baseline is not a strawman" step, and it is the step most ablation papers skip.
4. **Define the domain-upsampled (DU) mixture** by zeroing the largest bucket and rescaling: Large-Scale CC 0%, Small-Scale CC 30%, Domain Specific 35%, Code 35% (Table 4). No blending, no ramp — a hard switch at the seam.
5. **Branch from a mid-run checkpoint and finish the run under the new mixture.** For the 20% arm: take the 0.8T checkpoint, swap the mixture, train the remaining 0.2T. The total budget stays 1T; the intervention is FLOP-free. Learning rate schedule unchanged.
6. **Sweep the phase length** by branching from 0.95T / 0.9T / 0.8T / 0.7T for the 5% / 10% / 20% / 30% arms (50B / 100B / 200B / 300B tokens). Evaluate all arms on the same 35-task Gauntlet v0.3 (Table 6, Figure 3).
7. **Turn the phase into a dataset-attribution probe.** Fix the 10% arm's length and proportions, delete one candidate group (here, the math subsets) from the upsampled pool, rerun the 100B leg, and read the per-category damage off Gauntlet (Table 7). The claim is that this measures a dataset's contribution at a scale where the benchmark is not at its noise floor, for ~10% of a run.

## Assumptions

- **Assumption: the corpus decomposes into "diverse but low-density web" vs "small but information-dense domain data," and quality tracks density.** → Hides that our scarce lanes are not defined by density. **Indic** is a *language* axis, not a density axis: Indic web is both low-density *and* scarce, so "upsample the dense stuff, drop the web" has no defined action for it. **Agentic** trajectories are the opposite of dense — they are long, repetitive, low-entropy, and a 4.9x upsample of 0.63B unique tokens is pure repetition. The paper's mechanism simply does not have a slot for either.
- **Assumption: the anneal reserve is big enough to be upsampled ~5x without repeating.** → My derivation from their own epoch heuristic (see Numbers) shows their Domain Specific bucket ends the 20% arm at **0.89 epochs** and Code at **1.12 epochs** — they never repeat. Hides the entire question we face. Our anneal preset's agentic slot runs ~10 epochs *within the leg*; reason and long-context are fine (<0.2 epochs); Indic is fine on paper (0.08 epochs in the leg) but only because the leg is small. Any conclusion of theirs about "upsample 5x" is a conclusion about *reweighting*, not about *repeating*, and does not transfer to a lane that must repeat.
- **Assumption: a hard mixture switch at the seam is safe.** → They move 34.35 pp of mass to zero in one step and report no loss curve, no gradient norm, no spike, nothing. Hides exactly the V4 failure mode: our Hindi-share jump against frozen embeddings spiked gradient norm ~150x. Their model is monolingual English with a GPT-4 tiktoken vocabulary and no embedding freeze, so the seam was cheap for them and will not be for us. Their silence is not evidence of safety.
- **Assumption: the LR schedule is irrelevant to the result.** → They never vary it and never decay it. Hides whether the data gain survives (or grows, or shrinks) under an LR-to-zero cooldown. OLMo 2 later showed LR decay alone gives a comparable-magnitude gain, which means the two effects could be partly redundant. We should not budget +3.95 Gauntlet *on top of* whatever our LR decay already buys.
- **Assumption: one seed is enough.** → Not a single error bar, variance estimate, or repeated run anywhere in the paper. Hides that their two headline sub-claims — "MMLU peaks at 10%" (43.19 vs 42.59 at 20%, a **0.60 pp** gap) and "Core Average peaks at 20%" (39.32 vs 38.46 at 10%, a **0.86 pp** gap) — are differences of a size that a single seed cannot resolve. Cite the *shape* of the curve, never the exact peak.
- **Assumption: the sans-math arm isolates math's contribution.** → It does not, cleanly. They hold the *proportions* at 30/35/35 while shrinking the *pool* the 35% Domain-Specific slot draws from, so the surviving domain data is repeated harder in the sans-math arm than in the with-math arm. They never publish the token count of the removed math subsets, so the confound cannot be quantified. The finding "removing a lane can go below baseline" survives; the finding "math is worth exactly 13.48 pp of MMLU" does not.
- **Assumption: 4096 tokens of context and ALiBi are adequate.** → Hides long-context entirely. There is no long-document lane, no packing discussion, no RoPE scaling, nothing about how a long-CoT or long-trajectory reserve behaves when it is upsampled 5x at the end. Our anneal preset spends 8–10% on long-context and this paper has zero to say about it.
- **Assumption: benchmark contamination is not an issue.** → Never discussed. Llama 3, doing the same thing a year later, explicitly states "We do not include any training sets from commonly used benchmarks in our annealing data." Spark-joy makes no such statement, and an end-of-training upsample of curated data is precisely where contamination has maximum leverage on the score.

- **Hurt:** **Language Understanding is the only lane that is negative at every phase length** (−0.47 / −1.11 / −1.44 / −1.17). Its constituents are LAMBADA, HellaSwag, Winograd, Winogrande — coreference, cloze and fluency tasks, i.e. exactly the capabilities fed by the bulk web bucket that was zeroed. The paper waves it off as "only a small 1pp tradeoff." **For us this is the single most transferable warning against an 8% web anneal slot.**
- **Hurt by over-length:** at 30% vs their peak, MMLU −1.41, World Knowledge −1.01, Commonsense −0.78, Reading Comprehension **−2.95**, Core Average −0.43. Knowledge/comprehension decay while code/math keep climbing.
- **Hurt by omission:** everything except Programming, per the sans-math table above.

**Cost claim**
- The DU leg is **FLOP-free**: it is carved out of the 1T budget, not added. Each *additional* experimental arm costs only the branch length — 10% of a run for the 10% arm. "an order of magnitude lower cost compared to full pretraining runs" (abstract).
- Mapped to us: a 40B probe on 120B params ≈ **2.9e22 FLOPs** vs **1.44e24** for the full 2T run = **~1/50 of a run per probe**. [INFERENCE, 6ND arithmetic.]

**Evaluation harness**
- Gauntlet v0.3, **35 in-context-learning tasks** in 6 categories, random baseline subtracted and rescaled — `(0.3−0.25)/(1−0.25)=0.0667` is their worked example. Full task list in Appendix A / Table 8. MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval are reported raw. **Programming has exactly one constituent task, HumanEval**, so "Programming" and "HumanEval" are the same number throughout.

## Implementation nits

- **Architecture:** decoder-only transformer, MPT (LLM Foundry, `github.com/mosaicml/llm-foundry`), 7B params. **ALiBi** positional bias, not RoPE — relevant because ALiBi was chosen for length extrapolation, yet no long-context experiment appears in the paper.
- **Tokenizer:** tiktoken (GPT-4 vocab, ~100k BPE). English-centric; would be a poor choice for our Indic lane (high fertility on Devanagari and Dravidian scripts).
- **Optimizer:** **LionW**, LR **1.2e-4**, betas (0.9, 0.95), weight decay **1.2e-4**. Note LR and WD are numerically identical, which for decoupled Lion means the effective decay per step is LR·WD.
- **Schedule:** "inverse square root learning schedule similar to (Zhai et al. 2022)." No warmup length, no final-cooldown length, no LR value at the seam is stated. Zhai's rsqrt schedule *optionally* includes a linear cooldown; the paper does not say whether one was used. **[INFERENCE]** Under a pure rsqrt, LR(1.0T)/LR(0.8T) = √0.8 ≈ **0.894**, so the DU leg ran at ~89–100% of the seam LR — a data-only intervention, no annealing in the LR sense. **This ambiguity is the single biggest gap between their setup and our "low LR" anneal.**
- **Sequence length 4096, batch size 960** → **3,932,160 tokens per optimizer step**; 1T tokens ≈ **254,313 steps**; the 20% DU leg ≈ **50,863 steps**; the 5% leg ≈ **12,716 steps**.
- **Packing:** not discussed at all. No statement about document concatenation, EOS handling, intra-document masking, or whether short documents are packed. Given "Max Sequence Length 4096" the implied default in LLM Foundry is concatenate-and-chunk, but the paper does not say so.
- **Mixture transition:** hard switch at the seam. No ramp, no blend, no warmup of the new proportions, no re-warmup of the LR. No loss curve, no gradient-norm trace, and no perplexity number appears anywhere in the paper — only benchmark scores.
- **Evaluation:** Mosaic Eval Gauntlet v0.3, 35 tasks, six categories, random-baseline-subtracted then rescaled to [0,1] before aggregation. Shot counts vary per task (MMLU 5-shot, GSM8K 8-shot, HumanEval pass@1, PIQA 10-shot, etc.; full list Table 8). Gauntlet is a Mosaic-internal aggregate — the category numbers are not directly comparable to any other paper's "average."
- **Artifacts:** "we will release our models and intermediate checkpoints as research artifacts." No URL, no HuggingFace link, no code link beyond LLM Foundry in the paper itself.
- **Local assets:** `session_5_era_v5/papers/spark-joy/` contains `paper.pdf` (13 pp), `src/` (full arXiv TeX source incl. `main.tex` and the three figure PDFs), and the three rendered figure PNGs.

## Follow-on papers

- **2407.21783 — The Llama 3 Herd of Models.** Cites this paper by name ("Similar to Blakeney et al. 2024, we find that annealing enables us to judge the value of small domain-specific datasets") and industrialises the probe: **"we measure the value of such datasets by annealing the learning rate of a 50% trained Llama 3 8B model linearly to 0 on 40B tokens... we assign 30% weight to the new dataset and the remaining 70% weight to the default data mix."** *Why it changes a V5 number:* **40B is exactly our 2% figure**, and the 30/70 new-to-default ratio is a far gentler reweight than spark-joy's 41% mixture shift — it is a concrete, production-validated template for a short anneal. It also reports the effect is **size-dependent**: annealing on GSM8k/MATH train sets improved the 8B by 24.0% and 6.4% respectively but "the improvements on the 405B model are negligible." At 120B we are much closer to 405B than to 8B, which is a direct threat to our anneal's expected payoff. Separately, Llama 3 405B's *production* anneal is only the final **40M** tokens with LR→0 plus Polyak checkpoint averaging — three orders of magnitude shorter than spark-joy's recommendation, because it is doing a different job (LR landing, not capability injection).
- **2501.00656 — OLMo 2 Furious.** Also cites this paper, and is the paper that actually settles our 2%-vs-4% question. Mid-training is **"5–10% of training FLOPs"**, with concrete budgets: **OLMo 2 7B anneals 3× on 50B tokens from a 4T checkpoint (1.25% of the run)**; **13B and 32B use 3× 100B plus 1× 300B from 5T/7T checkpoints (2% and 6%)**; final model is the **average (soup) of those checkpoints**. Their mid-training mix holds **filtered DCLM web at ~50% of the budget in all three sizes** (Table 13). They also isolate the two halves of an "anneal": **LR decay alone (PT-mix, no data change) is worth +4.4 OLMES**, and swapping in higher-quality data adds more on top. And their *microanneal* result — 19 probes totalling 130B tokens, with "results visible after training for less than 10B tokens," 35/65 math/web giving GSM* 63.5 vs 10/90 giving 61.0, and 2x duplication of scarce math data *beating* 1x (66 vs 61) — is the closest thing in the literature to a defence of repeating a scarce lane in the anneal. *Why it changes a V5 number:* it is what makes a 2% (40B) anneal defensible at all, and it directly contradicts spark-joy's "10–20% of the run" on a fraction basis while agreeing with it on an absolute-token basis.
- **2404.06395 — MiniCPM (Warmup-Stable-Decay).** The other origin point for "high-quality data belongs in the decay phase," and the one that makes the LR-schedule case explicitly. *Why it changes a V5 number:* it would settle whether our anneal's low-LR leg and its curated-reserve leg have to be the same leg, which spark-joy leaves completely open by never touching the LR.
- **2406.11794 — DataComp-LM (Li et al. 2024b), cited by Llama 3 as the source of its annealing-mixture recipe.** *Why it changes a V5 number:* it is where the "upsample high-quality data in select domains during anneal" weights that Llama 3 adopted actually come from, and it publishes named datasets — which spark-joy conspicuously does not.
- **Not worth chasing for a pretrain number:** Zhai et al. 2022 (rsqrt schedule, vision), Chen et al. 2024 (Lion), Press et al. 2022 (ALiBi), Xie et al. 2024 (DoReMi — already superseded for our purposes by CLIMB).

## Assignment mapping

**1. Budget share for every capability lane (code / agentic / reasoning / long-context / Indic / STEM / general web, sum 100%).** — **Supports the shape, challenges our web number.** They publish both vectors with token counts: base `34.35 / 36.70 / 7.17 / 21.78` (Table 2) and anneal `0 / 30 / 35 / 35` (Table 4). But their lanes are quality tiers, not capabilities, and the usable sentence is the one that constrains us: *"We also maintain Small-Scale Common Crawl at high percentage to prevent a large distribution shift in our pretraining data"* — filtered web stays at **30%** in the anneal, moving only 0.82x. **Our anneal preset puts total web at 8%, a 0.24x cut.** The defensible reconciliation is to split our `web` lane into raw-crawl and filtered-HQ-web and zero only the raw half, keeping filtered web near 20–30% of the anneal. Also citable: their **21.78% code** in the main mix, justified as "a high percentage of code around 20% boosted programming and reasoning ability without negatively impacting language abilities" — that brackets our 24%.
**2. Indic split across verified / unverified / translated / synthetic.** — **Silent, and worse than silent.** The corpus is monolingual English, the tokenizer is tiktoken GPT-4, there is no multilingual evaluation in the 35-task Gauntlet, and no synthetic data anywhere. The paper's core mechanism ("upsample information-dense domain data relative to CC") has no defined action for a language lane, because Indic web is *both* the low-density bucket *and* the scarce bucket. Nothing here supports or challenges 40/25/20/15. Do not cite this paper for any Indic number.
**3. Agentic, reasoning, long-context as first-class pretrain slots.** — **Silent on agentic and long-context; indirectly supports reasoning.** Context is 4096 with ALiBi; no long documents, no packing discussion, no trajectory data, no tool-use data. For reasoning the usable evidence is the sans-math ablation: *"these specific datasets are responsible for the majority of the mathematical knowledge and reasoning capabilities in both the base model and the domain upsampled variant"* — with MMLU **43.19 → 29.71** and GSM8K **20.47 → 11.37** when they are pulled. A reasoning lane that owns its benchmarks is justified; nothing here justifies an agentic or long-context lane.
**4. Protected always-on floor the OPUS selector cannot starve.** — **Strongly supports. This is the paper's best gift to us.** *"every Gauntlet v0.3 subcategory score for the domain upsampling sans-math with the exception of programming is lower than the baseline model."* MMLU **29.71 vs 35.69 baseline (−5.98)**, Reading Comprehension **26.48 vs 37.02 (−10.54)**, Commonsense **31.76 vs 38.38 (−6.62)** — all from a **100B-token** final phase, 10% of the run. The floor argument is therefore stronger than "starving a lane forfeits its gains": **starving a lane in the final phase can drive it below where it already was.** The corollary our README must state is that the floor applies to the *anneal mixture* too, not only to the main-run selector.
**5. Anneal reserve: which best data is held back for the cooldown.** — **Strongly supports; it is the whole paper.** The reserve is `Domain Specific (35%) + Code (35%)`, with filtered web at 30% as ballast and raw web at 0%. Two derived constraints worth putting in the README: their **Domain Specific bucket peaks at 0.89 epochs and Code at 1.12 epochs across the entire run even in the best arm** — the reserve is *reweighted, never repeated*; and the reserve is **10.5% of their total unique corpus (361.2B of 3423.7B)**, which is the same order as ours (~11%).
**6. Each lane tied to the benchmarks it is meant to win.** — **Supports, with a clean attribution table.** Math/domain data → MMLU (+7.50), GSM8K (+5.76 at the same 10% point), Reading Comprehension (+6.33) — and all three collapse below baseline when math is removed. Code → HumanEval (+6.17 at 20%, and **+3.92 above baseline even in the sans-math arm**, i.e. code carries HumanEval by itself). Raw web → Language Understanding, the only category that is **negative at every phase length (−0.47 / −1.11 / −1.44 / −1.17)**, whose tasks are LAMBADA / HellaSwag / Winograd / Winogrande. That last one is the lane-to-benchmark tie our 8% web slot has to answer for.
**7. Demand vs unique token supply.** — **Supports methodologically, and indicts our agentic anneal slot.** *"We set the proportions for mixing these datasets based on a rough heuristic for the number of epochs each of these groups would be seen during the 1 trillion token training duration. Specifically, we choose 0.5 epochs for the Small-Scale Common Crawl and Domain Specific data and 1 epoch for Code. The remainder of the 1 trillion tokens are filled with Large-Scale Common Crawl."* That is supply-first weighting: pick epochs, derive percentages, backfill with the abundant bucket. Applied to our anneal preset at 80B (4%): reason 14.4B / 85B unique = 0.17 ep, long-ctx 6.4B / 100B = 0.06 ep, Indic 22.4B / 276B = 0.08 ep, STEM 8B / 250B = 0.03 ep — all comfortably inside their discipline. **Agentic 6.4B against 0.63B unique = ~10.2 epochs inside the anneal leg alone** (and ~5.1 epochs even at a 2% / 40B leg). By spark-joy's own rule that slot is not a reserve, it is a 10x repeat, and the README must either shrink it or state plainly that it is filled with freshly synthesised trajectories.

**Where it contradicts an already-established finding.** Two-Phase (2412.15285) concluded P2 should be **10–50% of the run, best 40%**. Spark-joy finds **30% is already past the peak** for MMLU, World Knowledge, Commonsense, Reading Comprehension and the Core Average. These are reconcilable, and the reconciliation is useful: spark-joy's phase-2 mixture is far more aggressive — it deletes 34.35 pp of the corpus outright, a **41.1%** mixture shift. **Hypothesis worth stating in the README: phase length and phase aggressiveness trade off — the more mass you move, the shorter the phase must be.** Our anneal moves **32.0%** of the mass, close to spark-joy's 41.1% and well above OpenCoder's (which kept 84% of the original distribution). That argues for a *short* anneal, i.e. it is an argument *for* our 2–4%, and it comes from taking spark-joy's aggressiveness seriously rather than its length recommendation.

**The direct answer to "is ~2% vs ~4% defensible?"** They **did** sweep phase length — four points, 5 / 10 / 20 / 30% = 50 / 100 / 200 / 300B — so the sweep exists and is citable. But **the shortest arm is 50B, and there is no measurement anywhere below it.** On a fraction-of-run reading their recommendation maps to **200–400B at 2T**, which kills both our options. On an absolute-token reading their knee is at **50–100B**, which contains our **4% (80B)** and excludes our **2% (40B)**. The absolute-token reading is the correct one, and OLMo 2 is the evidence: production anneals of **50B on a 4T run (1.25%)** and **100B on a 5T run (2%)** at 7B–32B scale. **Recommendation: set the anneal at 4% / 80B as the defensible floor**, cite spark-joy's 50B and 100B points as the bracket and OLMo 2's 50B/100B production anneals as the fraction-independence evidence; if the budget is cut to 2% / 40B, cite Llama 3's 40B anneal instead and expect, on spark-joy's interpolated curve, to give up roughly 2–3 pp of MMLU and GSM8K relative to 4%.

## What not to overclaim

- **Do not claim "10–20% is optimal" as if it were resolved, and do not claim it transfers to 2T.** MMLU's "peak at 10%" is 43.19 against 42.59 at 20% — a **0.60 pp** gap, single seed, no error bars anywhere in the paper. The Core Average "peak at 20%" is a **0.86 pp** gap. The defensible claim is the curve *shape*: a large jump by 50B, a plateau through 100–200B, and general-capability decay past 200B. Worse, with only one run length (1T) the paper **cannot distinguish** whether the optimum is a fraction of the run or an absolute token count — and OLMo 2's 1.25–2% production anneals on 4–5T runs strongly suggest it is absolute. Citing "10–20%" against our 2T budget would be citing the one reading the follow-on literature contradicts.
- **Do not claim this paper validates a low-LR anneal, or that its gains add to ours.** It is a **pure data intervention at cruising learning rate** — inverse-sqrt schedule, no decay mentioned at the seam, LR falling by at most ~11% across the entire final leg. Our anneal bundles a mixture swap *and* an LR decay. OLMo 2 measured the LR decay alone at +4.4 OLMES, so some unknown fraction of spark-joy's +3.95 Gauntlet may be redundant with what our cooldown already buys. Budget the two effects as overlapping, not additive.
- **Do not claim it says anything about Indic, agentic, long-context, or synthetic data, and do not claim the seam is safe.** Monolingual English, 4096-token context with ALiBi, zero synthetic data, no dataset named beyond "Wikipedia", no token counts for the subsets it ablates. It also reports **no loss curve, no perplexity, and no gradient norm** — so its hard 34-point mixture switch tells us nothing about whether our seam is stable, and specifically nothing that bears on the V4 incident where a sudden Hindi increase against frozen embeddings spiked gradient norm ~150x. Their silence on the seam is absence of measurement, not evidence of safety; keep the multi-billion-token blend.
