# Demystifying Synthetic Data in LLM Pre-training: A Systematic Study of Scaling Laws, Benefits, and Pitfalls
arxiv:2510.01631v1 [cs.LG], 2 Oct 2025 (HTML date line). FAIR at Meta / Virginia Tech / Cerebras / independent. Authors: Feiyang Kang, Newsha Ardalani, Michael Kuchnik, Youssef Emad, Mostafa Elhoushi, Shubhabrata Sengupta, Shang-Wen Li, Ramya Raghavendra, Ruoxi Jia, Carole-Jean Wu.
Read via: **HTML first and it worked** (`https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01631` = v1; local `session_5_era_v5/papers/synthetic/paper.html`). PDF used for figure pixels (`paper.pdf` + `figs/`). TeX not needed. WRAP (2401.16380) HTML skimmed only for the 1:1 mix this paper contradicts.

## Teach this first

High-quality natural text is finite. This paper asks a narrower question than “should we synthesize everything?”: if you pre-train from scratch on English CommonCrawl, does adding model-written text help, hurt, or only help in a certain mix? They answer with scaling laws, not a single run. Over 1000 decoder-only models (100M–3B non-embedding params, up to 200B tokens, >100k GPU hours) are trained under one protocol so mixture, type, model size, and budget can be compared. **Why V5 cares:** V5 already plans to synthesize the agentic 63× hole (40B demand vs 0.63B unique) and to put 15% of the Indic lane in a D-tier “synthetic” bucket. This paper is the best public measurement of *safe synth:real ratios* in pre-training, and it is not a blank check.

They study two families of synthetic text. *Rephrasing* (WRAP-style) takes a real CommonCrawl document and asks a 7B instructor to rewrite it, either as clean Wikipedia-like prose (HQ) or as Question/Answer pairs (QA). The facts stay; the style changes. *Textbooks* (Phi / Cosmopedia-style) invent a 10-chapter book from CC keywords, then write each chapter from the outline only. That is new content, not a rewrite. **Why V5 cares:** Indic D and the agentic hole are not the same object. Rephrasing needs a real source document. Filling 40B agent tokens from 0.63B unique trajectories is textbook-style invention unless you have real traces to rewrite.

The headline is not “synthetic is better.” Pure HQ or QA is *not* faster than raw CommonCrawl. Pure textbooks are *worse*, especially at small budgets, with the collapse-like pattern of high downstream loss. The win is a **mixture**: about one-third high-quality rephrased text plus two-thirds natural web can reach the same validation loss **5–10× faster** at large data budgets, and that mix has the lowest projected irreducible loss. **Why V5 cares:** a 15% Indic-D slice is conservative if D is rephrased verified Indic. A 63× invented agentic corpus is the setting this paper says looks like collapse.

“Good” mix is type- and scale-dependent, and it **converges below 50%**. HQ rephrase is stably ~30% synth / 70% CC. QA wants ~50% at tiny models and slides toward 30% as model and data grow. Textbooks want almost none (pixels: ≤3% at 50B tokens, often 0% below 10B). WRAP’s 1:1 recipe loses to 30% here. Larger models are *less* tolerant of high synth fractions than larger *data* budgets are. **Why V5 cares:** do not copy WRAP’s 50% or Phi’s “textbooks are all you need.” For a multi-billion V5 run, treat 30% rephrase as a ceiling on that style, and treat textbook-style as a spice, not a lane.

Bigger generators are not automatically better pre-training teachers. Llama-3-8B rephrases beat Llama-3-3B. Llama-3-70B does **not** beat 8B on HQ (pixels: 8B is lowest across the HQ sweep; 70B is worst at 0.5–2%). An 8B-class instructor is enough; paying for 70B generation is wasted for Wikipedia-style rephrase. **Why V5 cares:** Indic and agentic synthesis should be budgeted at ~8B generator cost, not “use the biggest teacher we have.”

They try to explain the mix with unigram statistics. CommonCrawl covers more tokens and is closest (lowest KL) to the English Pile/Wikitext test mix — and still loses to the 30% HQ mix. Missing tokens (`.\n\n`, stray Danish/Turkish pieces) spike per-token loss, but no single corpus covers everything. Quality vs diversity is a real trade, not “match the test unigrams.” **Why V5 cares:** Indic and agentic test distributions are *not* The Pile. A synth mix that looks good on English web loss can still punch a hole in Hindi morphology or tool-call syntax.

What they do **not** claim: multilingual, code, math, agent traces, long context, or downstream benchmarks. The metric is English per-token CE on 14 non-code/math Pile domains plus Wikitext-103. Scale stops at 3B / 200B (HQ/QA unique synth even stop at 100B) with a fit then extrapolated to 8T / 400B. One generation of synth (n=1), one epoch, no anneal stage. **Why V5 cares:** cite the 30% / 5–10× / “don’t do pure textbooks” numbers; do not cite this paper as proof that 40B of invented agent trajectories will work.

## Method, in order

1. **Natural baseline.** Unfiltered English CommonCrawl through RedPajama-v2 (Weber et al. 2024). This is the “web” they mix against — not FineWeb, not a quality-filtered crawl.
2. **Generator.** One frozen instructor for the main corpus: Mistral-Instruct-7B-v0.1. Sample CC documents as sources/seeds. Decode at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95. Light heuristics drop docs <50 tokens or wildly over target length.
3. **HQ rephrase.** Prompt: rewrite the document as a full Wikipedia-style article, same content, no extra notes. Source truncated to ~2k tokens; output ~550 tokens average.
4. **QA rephrase.** Prompt: convert the same document into a complete list of `Question:` / `Answer:` pairs covering all information. Also ~550 tokens average. This is their stand-in for “instruction pre-training” (Cheng et al. 2024).
5. **TXBK (textbooks).** Two-step. (i) From CC keywords/themes, write a 10-chapter outline plus title/intro/appendices, in one of four audience voices (grade school / college / experts / general). (ii) Write each chapter from the outline only (~450 tokens/chapter, ~5k tokens/book), with exercises and reference answers. Content is invented, not grounded in the source page.
6. **Mixtures (main grid).** For each type independently: 100% CC, 100% synth, 67% synth / 33% CC, 33% synth / 67% CC. Concatenate and shuffle. 3–5+ seeds per cell. HQ and QA unique pools stop at 100B tokens; TXBK and CC go to 200B for the hold-out.
7. **Student models.** Llama-3-like decoder-only (SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE), sizes by *non-embedding* params: 100M (d=576, L=7, H=9), 200M (832/10/13), 500M (1280/16/20), 1B (1792/22/28), 2B (2240/28/35), 3B (2624/32/41). Meta Lingua + PyTorch, from scratch, 1 epoch.
8. **Train.** AdamW β1=0.9, β2=0.95 (App. A.2.2). Table 2: peak LR 3.0×10⁻⁴, 10% warmup, min LR 1.0×10⁻⁶, grad clip 1.0, seq 4096, per-device batch 4, accum 8, claimed effective batch 1M tokens, Llama-3 TikToken 128k, bf16, BOS+EOS, 8×A100 80GB. Cosine schedule stated in §4.1.2. Weight decay is **not** listed in Table 2. ~15B tokens/day for a 1B model on one node.
9. **Eval.** Final-checkpoint per-token CE / log-ppl, averaged on 14 *non-code/math English* Pile domains (NIH ExPorter, Pile-CC, Wikipedia-en, USPTO, PubMed Central/Abstracts, PhilPapers, OpenWebText2, OpenSubtitles, Gutenberg PG-19, FreeLaw, BookCorpus2, Books3, ArXiv) plus Wikitext-103. No MMLU / HumanEval / agent bench in the scaling laws.
10. **Scaling laws.** Data (1B student, D from 1B–200B): L̂(D)=B/D^β+E, fit ≤100B, check at 200B (RMABE 0.41%), then draw to 8T. Model (50B tokens, N from 100M–3B): L̂(N)=A/N^α+E, fit ≤2B, check at 3B (RMABE 0.30%), then draw to 400B. Joint: L̂(N,D)=A/N^α+B/D^β+E on ~700 runs; E is irreducible loss.
11. **Fine ratio search.** Extra ~400 models (200M–1B, 1B–50B tokens). Synth fraction in {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 50, 100}%. “Good” = lowest val loss.
12. **Generator ablation.** Rebuild HQ/QA subsets with Llama-3-3B / 8B / 70B. Train a fixed 1B student for 5B tokens at synth fractions {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20}%. ~200 runs.
13. **Unigram autopsy.** Zipf fits, train–test KL, token-frequency overlays, and per-token eval loss (1B / 100B tokens) to see whether synth wins by matching the test distribution (it does not).

## Assumptions

- **English unfiltered CC is the right natural prior** → hides Indic morphology, code, tool JSON, and long agent traces. A 30% English-rephrase win does not transfer to Hindi or function-calling without a new grid.
- **Rephrase preserves facts / TXBK is “educational”** → no factuality audit. TXBK can invent confident false textbooks. Dangerous if Indic D or STEM is generated this way.
- **Loss on English Pile/Wikitext is the objective** → silent on Indic benches, SWE-Bench, long-context, agent success. They say so in Limitations.
- **One-round (n=1) synth, one epoch, no recursive web** → does not test multi-generation collapse or “the web is already synth-contaminated.”
- **Student ≤3B, unique HQ/QA ≤100B** → 8T / 400B curves are extrapolations. V5 at 1–10T and >>3B is off the measured surface; they flag >100B / >10T as unvalidated.
- **Mixture is a static shuffle, not a curriculum** → no seed / anneal / cooldown schedule. Cannot defend holding HQ back for the last 2%.
- **Generator ~7–8B is enough, 70B is not better** → measured only for English rephrase of CC, 1B student, 5B tokens. Unknown for Indic or long agent trajectories.
- **Tokenizer is Llama-3 128k English-heavy** → they already see high loss on uncovered unigrams (`.\n\n`, Danish `hvor`, Turkish `dön`). Indic D plus a frozen English tokenizer is exactly the V4 Hindi × frozen-embedding failure mode, and this paper does not test it.

## Implementation nits

- Code promised (“open-sourcing our full recipe”) but no URL in the paper. Training stack: [facebookresearch/lingua](https://github.com/facebookresearch/lingua).
- Student sizes are non-embedding counts (Kaplan convention). Tokenizer is the *pretrained* Llama-3 128k TikToken, not trained on this mix.
- Batch: 4 × 4096 × 8 accum = 131,072 tokens/step on the 8×A100 row, vs the table’s “1M effective” claim — treat 1M as the intended global batch (more GPUs/microbatches than one node). Peak LR 3e-4 for all sizes. Weight decay not in Table 2.
- HQ/QA generation is document-conditioned; TXBK chapter generation is outline-conditioned only. Four TXBK audience prompts are the only diversity knob.
- Fine grid is exponential and **skips 30%** (points are 20% and 50%). The “~30%” headline is interpolated from the 20-vs-50 comparison plus the coarse 33% cell, not a measured 30% point.
- Eval Table 3 “Max Tokens to Generate = 2048” is leftover generator config; the actual metric is teacher-forced CE.
- English-only. Code/math Pile slices deliberately dropped from the average.
- Scale-statement inconsistency: Abstract / §1 say >1000 models and >100k GPU hours; §4.1 says ~600 variants and >70k GPU hours for the main grid. The extra ~400 (ratio search) + ~200 (generator) reconcile the totals.

## Assignment mapping

1. **Budget share for every capability lane (code / agentic / reason / long / Indic / STEM / web = 100%).** **Silent.** Only English CC vs HQ/QA/TXBK. No lane weights, no code, no Indic, no long-ctx. Cannot defend 24/2/6/6/16/12/34 from this paper.

2. **Indic split A verified 40 / B unverified 25 / C translated 20 / D synthetic 15.** **Challenges D if D is Phi-style textbooks; weakly supports D if D is rephrase of real Indic.** Quote: “Best-found ratios are all below 50% [and] appear to converge ~30%” for rephrase; TXBK “optimal ratios are often minimal (<5%) for smaller configurations” (§5.1). Pixels put TXBK even lower (≤3.1% at 50B). 15% of the Indic lane is 2.4% of the 2T run — fine as *rephrased* verified Indic, reckless as invented textbooks. The paper never measures Hindi/Indic and never splits verified vs translated.

3. **Agentic, reasoning, long-context as first-class slots pointed at inventory.** **Silent** on long-context and on real agent inventories. **Weak / hostile on the intended recipes:** QA rephrase is their proxy for instruction pre-training, but it is still a rewrite of CC, not tool traces. TXBK was *meant* to buy reasoning/coding and then *hurt* English val loss; code/math were excluded from the metric. Do not point the reasoning or agentic slot at Cosmopedia-style books on this citation.

4. **Protected always-on floor OPUS cannot starve (Indic + agentic + reasoning).** **Silent** on OPUS. Indirect support for “never let natural mass go to zero”: every 100% synth cell is worse or no better, and 67% TXBK loses to CC. A floor that keeps real Indic / real traces in every batch is consistent; a floor made of synthetic stand-ins is not what they measured.

5. **Anneal reserve: which best data is held back for cooldown.** **Silent.** One stage, one epoch, no LR-cooldown recipe. Related-work pointer only: Meta (2025) mid-trains synthetic reasoning *after* pretrain. Their own model-scaling result (synth less helpful as N grows) mildly argues *against* raising synth at the end of a large run, except that TXBK’s relative value grew with *data* budget, not model size.

6. **Each lane tied to the benchmarks it is meant to win.** **Silent / challenges the method.** Scaling is CE on Pile-English + Wikitext. Limitations: “Additional evaluations on NLP benchmarks would be a desirable addition as loss is not our final goal.” Cannot fund MMLU / Indic suites / agent benches from these curves.

7. **Demand vs unique token supply; where a share only works by repeating or synthesizing.** **Supports rephrase-as-efficiency; challenges 63× invented agentic.** Unique HQ/QA pools stop at 100B — even Meta could not (or did not) materialize infinite rephrase. “Pre-training on rephrased synthetic data *alone* is not faster than … natural web texts; [but] 1/3 rephrased … mixed with 2/3 natural … can speed up 5–10×” (Abstract). That is a way to get more *effective* tokens from existing unique docs, not a license to mint 40B agent tokens from 0.63B unique. “Training on mixtures of textbook-style pure-generated synthetic data shows patterns predicted by model collapse” (Abstract, §7). A 63× TXBK-like expansion of agentic is exactly the cell they say looks like collapse.

## What not to overclaim

- Do not write “synthetic pre-training is 5–10× better.” The 5–10× is **tokens-to-equal-loss** for **~30% HQ/QA rephrase + 70% CC**, on **English Pile CE**, for **≤3B** students, with the big end **extrapolated**. Pure synth does not get it. Pixel iso-loss in the measured 10–100B band is closer to 4–6×.
- Do not use this paper to bless Indic D-tier textbooks or a 63× agentic generator. Those are TXBK-class, English-unmeasured, and the TXBK cells are the failure case (Fig. 4 right never leaves the 0–3% band at these scales).
- Do not treat the 8T / 400B dashed curves, or the irreducible-loss bars, as measurements. They are fits from ≤200B / ≤3B. The authors say frontier (>100B params, >10T tokens) is unvalidated. Fig. 3’s “Best” arrow on HQ33 is not separable from QA33 on the pixels.
