## Verdict for our assignment

**High value, but only as a normalization layer — not as evidence.** This survey runs no experiments and reports no ablation of its own; every number in it is second-hand. What it uniquely gives us is the thing the V5 README most needs and no single primary paper provides: **Table II, a normalized "General / Annealing / Long-context — (# phases, # tokens)" ledger for 17 named frontier runs**, which lets us compute the mid-train share of total tokens for each and settle the OpenCoder-4%-vs-Two-Phase-40% dispute quantitatively. Yes, **citable anneal numbers exist**: named runs with anneal token counts (MiniCPM 20B/1T, Hunyuan-Large 350B/7T, Pangu 0.4T/9.6T, OLMo-2 ~600B/5T, Qwen3 5T/30T, Zamba 1T/950B, MiMo 4T/18T), disclosed anneal *mixtures* (SmolLM2 58/24/14/4; Zamba 60/40; Pangu +20% instruction +18% STEM; Hunyuan "top 5% highest-quality tokens"), and a WSD prescription that the decay leg is ~10% of total tokens.

Three caveats that must travel with every citation. (1) **Cite the primary, not the survey** — I found two hard errors: Table II lists LLaMA3-405B annealing as "1 (40B)" while the survey's own §III-B and §IV-C say "final 40M tokens"; the Llama 3 paper (2407.21783 §3.4.3) confirms **40M** for the 405B terminal anneal and **40B** for a separate *8B* data-valuation probe (§3.1.3) — the survey conflated two different experiments 1000× apart. And Granite 3.0's Stage-1 mix as printed ("5% Web, 11% Domain, 10% Code, 10% Math, 10% Instruction, 5% Multilingual, 5% Academic, 4% Technical") **sums to 60%**; the arXiv source's commented-out earlier draft says "45% Web", which sums to 100. (2) **The single most important number in the paper is negative for us**: "Annealing improved Llama 3 8B on GSM8k by 24.0% and MATH by 6.4%, but a negligible uplift for 405B model" (Table VII). Every large anneal uplift in the survey comes from a ≤14B model; the one ≥100B datapoint says the anneal did essentially nothing. Our run is 120B. (3) **The taxonomy has no Indic/multilingual lane and no agentic lane at all.** Figure 1's legend enumerates exactly eight mid-training data types — long, FIM, reasoning, synthetic, instruction, math, code, web — and multilingual is not one of them. "Agentic" appears nowhere; the string `agent` occurs once in the whole source, as the dataset `Agent-FLAN` inside MAP-Neo's *instruction* column. Our two protected lanes are the two the field has not written down.

## Teach this first

**1. "Mid-training" is the name for the last leg of the same pretraining run, and it is now a standard stage, not a trick.** The survey's claim is that state-of-the-art runs no longer go pretrain → SFT; they go pretrain → *one or more annealing-style phases* → SFT, where the annealing phases keep the identical next-token objective but change three things at once: the data mixture gets curated, the learning rate is decayed, and the context window is extended. The abstract calls this "multiple annealing-style phases that refine data quality, adapt optimization schedules, and extend context length." Figure 1 dates the practice: all 18 models on the timeline (2024-02 → 2025-05) carry a mid-training annotation, and the ones missing a piece of the pattern are concentrated early (Nemotron-4 15B, MAP-Neo, Granite 3.0, Zamba-v1 and OLMo-2 have no long-context leg at all; Nemotron-4 15B carries no LR-decay glyph). **Why it matters for V5:** our lifecycle split — pretrain ~95% of tokens with CE on every token, anneal ~2% with the same CE at low LR on a curated reserve — is the mainstream architecture of a 2025 run, not an invention we have to defend from scratch. What we do have to defend is the specific *sizes*.

**2. There are two different things people call "the anneal", and confusing them is where the 4%-vs-40% fight comes from.** Table II splits mid-training into three named slots — **General**, **Annealing**, **Long-context** — each with a phase count and a token count. When you compute anneal-tokens ÷ total-tokens across the table, the runs fall into two clean clusters. **Cluster A, the terminal fast-decay leg (1–5%)**: MiniCPM 20B on 1.02T = 1.96%; Hunyuan-A13B 300B on 20.3T = 1.48%; OpenCoder 100B on 2.6T = 3.85%; Pangu's annealing phase 0.4T on 10.0T = 4.00%; Hunyuan-Large 350B on 7.36T = 4.76%; OLMo-2's individual 100B run on 5.1T = 1.96%. **Cluster B, a full second stage that rewrites the distribution (9–51%)**: SmolLM2 1T on 11.075T = 9.03%; Nemotron-4 1T on 9T = 11.11%; AFM 1T on 7.4T = 13.51%; Qwen3 5T on 35.5T = 14.08%; MAP-Neo 778B on 5.278T = 14.74%; Granite 3.0 2T on 12T = 16.67%; MiMo-7B 4T on 24T = 16.67%; Pangu's reasoning+annealing 3.4T on 13T = 26.15%; Zamba-v1 1T on 1.95T = 51.28%. Between them sits MiniCPM's WSD prescription: the decay leg should be "only 10% of total tokens" (§IV-B6). **Why it matters for V5:** OpenCoder's ~4% and Two-Phase's best-40% were never in conflict — they are Cluster A and Cluster B measurements of two different phases. Our "2T main + anneal on top at ~2%" is a Cluster-A cooldown, at the low edge but inside the observed band (MiniCPM 1.96%, OLMo-2 per-run 1.96%, Hunyuan-A13B 1.48% are all at or below it). Our five-stage *curriculum* (Seed→General→Reasoning→Long→Anneal) is the Cluster-B machinery, and there Qwen3's 5T reasoning-heavy stage (14%) and Pangu's 3T reasoning phase (23%) are the named precedents.

**3. Nobody rewrites the mixture wholesale in the anneal; the new data is a 10–40% overlay on the old distribution.** This is the strongest convergent pattern in the paper and it is stated five different ways by five different labs. OpenCoder "retains 84% of its mid-training data from the original pretraining distribution" — the survey's stated reason is that "a mismatch between mid-training and pretraining corpora can lead to catastrophic forgetting and degraded generalization" (§III-C). Zamba-v1's mid-train is "60% pretraining data and 40% high-quality datasets". Phi-4's mid-training is 75B curated long-context + 175B *recall* tokens from earlier stages = 30/70. Hunyuan-Large's long-text phase is "25% natural long-context data obtained from books and codes and 75% normal length pre-training data". Nemotron-4 uses two distributions where "the first distribution, which makes up the majority of this phase, consists of tokens already seen during pretraining but reweighted." And from the Llama 3 primary, the annealing probe assigns "30% weight to the new dataset and the remaining 70% weight to the default data mix." **Why it matters for V5:** this gives us a defensible, quotable metric for our anneal preset. Our anneal (20/8/18/8/28/10/8) vs main (24/2/6/6/16/12/34) moves exactly **32% of the probability mass** (positive deviations: agentic +6, reason +12, long +2, Indic +12 = 32; negative: web −26, code −4, STEM −2 = −32). 32% new / 68% continuity lands inside the 10–40% band, next to Zamba's 40% as the most aggressive precedent. That is the sentence to put in the README, and it survives a reviewer who has read OpenCoder.

**4. The anneal reserve is chosen by a quality *quantile*, not by a source list.** Hunyuan-Large is the cleanest statement: "During the learning rate annealing phase, the model is trained on 5% of the highest-quality pre-training tokens, which plays a pivotal role in augmenting the model's performance" (§III-B). Around it the survey lists the other selectors: learned/classifier filtering for web (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM-baseline, Ultra-FineWeb, peS2o); MiniMax-M1's "semantic deduplication and heuristic scoring to prioritize question-answer pairs and STEM-oriented data"; Nemotron-4's second distribution which "up-weights domains where the model underperforms"; OLMo-2's synthetic data "for strengthening weak capabilities"; and Pangu's "curriculum-based sampling and ablation with a 7B proxy to optimize data strategies." **Why it matters for V5:** Hunyuan's top-5% quantile is the closest published analogue to our OPUS selector (keep ~40% of candidates, ~6× effective tokens). It also tells us OPUS at 40% acceptance is *ten times looser* than the strictest disclosed anneal filter — which is an argument for a much tighter quantile on the anneal reserve specifically than on the main run. Pangu's 7B proxy independently corroborates CLIMB's cheap-proxy search at a frontier lab.

**5. Sequence-length extension is a *stage with its own budget*, and it is cheap — 0.1% to 8% of the run.** Computed from Table II: Hunyuan-Large 10B/7.36T = 0.14%; SmolLM2 75B/11.075T = 0.68%; DeepSeek-V3 120B (2×60B)/14.92T = 0.80%; AFM 100B/7.4T = 1.35%; Qwen3 ~500B/35.5T = 1.41%; Phi-4 250B/10.25T = 2.44%; LLaMA3-405B 800B/15.84T = 5.05%; MiMo-7B 2T/24T = 8.33%. The long-share *inside* that stage is 9–75%: InternLM2 "~9% mixed long sequences", Hunyuan-Large 25%, Phi-4 30%, SmolLM2 40%, Qwen-2.5-1M ~40%, Qwen3 75%. The survey's own summary is "mix long/short tokens (often 30–60% long)" (§V-D), because "training solely on long data can hurt performance, and a balanced mixture with high-quality short-context data is critical" (§III-C, from ProLong-8B and Phi-4), and "naturally long-form content outperforms artificially concatenated data." **Why it matters for V5:** we currently carry long-context as a 6% *lane* in a flat main mixture and 8–10% in the anneal. The field never does that. It gives long-context its own terminal stage with a window change, ~0.1–8% of tokens, and 30–60% long documents inside it. And Phi-4's finding kills concatenation-as-long-data: our long-context lane must be genuinely long documents (books, repos), not packed shorts.

**6. Ordering: the long-context stage almost always comes *after* the anneal, not before.** Counting Table II and §V-C: long-context follows the anneal in AFM (4k→8k anneal→32k), Hunyuan-Large, Hunyuan-A13B (fast anneal to 8k, then 32k, then 256k), SmolLM2, Qwen3, MiMo-7B, Yi-Lightning, MiniMax-M1, Phi-4 and MiniCPM-4 — ten runs. It precedes the anneal in exactly one: LLaMA3-405B extends 8K→128K over ~800B tokens in six stages and *then* anneals over the final 40M tokens at 128K. A third option exists: Pangu Pro MoE has no separate long stage and instead "is trained with 32K sequence length during the two annealing stages" (Table II footnote †). **Why it matters for V5:** our curriculum puts Long-ctx before Anneal, which is the minority order — but it is not unprecedented, and it is the *only* order compatible with an anneal preset that contains 10% long-context data, because you cannot spend reserved long agent trajectories at 4K. Cite Llama-3 (anneal at 128K after extension) or Pangu (32K held through the anneal) explicitly, and say which one we are copying.

**7. The uplift evidence is real, large, and almost entirely from small models.** Table VII: OLMo-2 mid-training improves the 7B by **10.6** average points and the 13B by **10.3**, with GSM8K **+43.4 / +37.8**, DROP **+20.1 / +21.1**, MMLU **+3.9 / +4.1**, ARC-Challenge **+7.2 / +3.3**. MiniCPM4 "achieves comparable performance to Qwen3 using 22% of tokens (8T vs 36T)". Gemma2-9B "shows up to 10% improvement in some benchmarks". Zamba's phase-1 base "achieves Llama2 performance with 1T tokens (Llama2 uses 2T)". FineMath4+ gives "a 2x improvement on GSM8K and a 6x improvement on MATH compared to InfiMM-WebMath" (§III-C). Then: Llama 3 8B +24.0% GSM8k / +6.4% MATH from annealing, "but a negligible uplift for 405B model." **Why it matters for V5:** we should budget the anneal as an *insurance and specialization* leg, not as a headline-benchmark lever, and we should say out loud that the expected uplift at 120B is unknown and plausibly small — the only frontier-scale measurement in the literature is null. The corollary is that the anneal's real value at our scale is the things Llama-3 kept it for anyway: upsampling the best data, extending the window, and Polyak-averaging checkpoints.

**8. Two mechanisms in here directly attack our scarcity problem, and one attacks our order-sensitivity problem.** First, **FIM as a token multiplier**: "FIM increases data efficiency by allowing multiple span-prediction opportunities from a single sample, making it well-suited for late-stage training where high-quality data is scarce" (§III-A) — and per Table II and Figure 1, DeepSeek-V3 is the *only* surveyed run using FIM in mid-training. Second, **repetition beats diversity for scarce high-value data**: "Repeating high-value data (e.g., math examples) a few times yields performance gains, with diminishing returns beyond a certain point... models trained on smaller repeated datasets can outperform those trained on larger, unrepeated datasets, especially in mathematical reasoning" (§III-C, crediting Charton et al.). Third, **model souping over data orders**: "OLMo 2 repeats mid-training with different random orders and averages the resulting models. The 7B model trains three runs of 50B tokens each, while the 13B model performs three runs of 100B tokens plus one of 300B tokens, averaging across all" (§III-B). **Why it matters for V5:** our agentic lane is short by ~63× (40B demand vs 0.63B unique). FIM span-masking over agent trajectories and unit-tested code is a legitimate, cited way to extract more gradient signal per unique token — nobody in the survey has done it for agentic data, which makes it a differentiator rather than a liability. And OLMo-2's souping is a cheap hedge against the one thing Two-Phase proved matters (order alone = +3.4 avg): if we cannot know the best order, run the anneal three times and average.

## Method, in order

This is a survey; the "method" is the taxonomy it imposes and the evidence pipeline it uses to fill it. Reconstructed:

1. **Scope mid-training** as the interval between general pretraining and SFT, comprising annealing-style phase(s) that share the pretraining objective. Justify it with three borrowed theories, none tested here: (i) *gradient noise scale* — higher-quality data induces greater gradient variance ⇒ higher GNS ⇒ escapes sharp minima (McCandlish et al., 1812.06162); (ii) *information bottleneck* — LR annealing progressively compresses reliance on noisy features (Tishby, Alemi); (iii) *curriculum learning* — the distribution shifts toward harder/more informative examples as the LR falls.
2. **Axis 1 — Data distribution.** Enumerate eight mid-training data types: HQ filtered web; code & math; instruction/QA; synthetic textbooks & knowledge-dense; long-context; reasoning & CoT; FIM. (Table II uses eight columns by splitting Math/STEM from code.) For each type, list representative corpora with citations. → **Table I** (5 models × 5 dataset columns, dataset-level provenance for MiniCPM, Zamba-v1, MAP-Neo, OLMo-2, SmolLM2).
3. **Normalize the run structures.** For 17 models, extract (# phases, # tokens) for General / Annealing / Long-context and tick which of the 8 data types appear in the annealing & long-context stages. → **Table II**. This is the single most reusable artifact in the paper.
4. **Narrate each run** in §III-B with whatever mixture percentages the primary disclosed (18 model paragraphs). This is where the mixtures live: SmolLM2 58/24/14/4, MAP-Neo 74.5/8.5/17, Nemotron 70/15/15, Llama3 50/17/25/8, Granite's 8 buckets, Zamba 60/40, Pangu +20%/+18%, MiMo ~70% math+code, MiniMax-M1 70% STEM+code+books+reasoning, Hunyuan top-5%.
5. **Distill 11 data insights** (§III-C), each attributed to a primary: quality > scale; educational/code/math boosts STEM; instruction-data placement unresolved; long-context needs source selection not just length; small domain fractions suffice; synthetic helps; reasoning data critical; moderate repetition and rewriting substitute for scale; distributional continuity prevents forgetting; overtraining hurts tunability.
6. **Axis 2 — LR schedules.** Define eight scheduler families with closed forms (linear, cosine, exponential, Knee, cyclical, **WSD**, **Power**, multi-step), plot them on one axis (Figure 2), then tabulate the actual decay shape, LR path, optimizer and staged-training flag for **31 named models** (Table III). Distill five insights (§IV-D), the honest one being that decay design "remains unsettled and system-dependent."
7. **Axis 3 — Long-context extension.** Derive RoPE, then unify PI / NTK-aware / NTK-by-parts / YaRN / LongRoPE as *one* object: a frequency remap `h(θ_j)` plus an optional length/temperature scale `α(s)` (Eq. 11; Table IV; Figure 3). Then tabulate max length, extension technique, architectural assist, and long-context dataset for **30 models** (Table V), grouped by technique family (native RoPE / ABF / YaRN / DCA-at-inference / LongRoPE / NTK-PI-NoPE / architecture-dominant). Emit a practical default (§V-D).
8. **Evaluation.** Fix a 15-benchmark panel by capability (Table VI) and collect reported mid-training gains for 15 models into three buckets — (A) Advanced Reasoning & Adaptability, (B) Scalable Efficiency, (C) Reliable Foundations (Table VII).
9. **Open challenges** (§VI): dynamic curricula that adapt composition from live signals (loss sharpness, gradient variance, benchmark outcomes); theoretically grounded scale-aware schedulers; long-context via learnable position functions + KV compression.

## Assumptions

- **Assumption: the disclosed numbers are comparable across runs.** → Hides that the "annealing" column of Table II mixes a 20B cooldown (MiniCPM), a 5T reasoning stage (Qwen3), and a sum over four *parallel* averaged runs (OLMo-2 13B: 3×100B + 1×300B = "~600B", which is not a 600B sequential phase). For V5: never quote a mid-train % without saying which phase kind it is, or a reviewer will pick the other cluster.
- **Assumption: the survey's transcription of primaries is correct.** → Hides two errors I verified (LLaMA3 "1 (40B)" vs 40M; Granite Stage-1 summing to 60%). For V5: every number in our README must be cited to the primary arXiv ID, with the survey as a finding aid only.
- **Assumption: mid-training gains observed at 1.7B–14B transfer upward.** → Hides that the only ≥100B measurement is null ("negligible uplift for 405B"). For V5 at 120B: our anneal ROI is unmeasured; do not promise benchmark deltas from it. Explicitly out of scope for the survey too — it never discusses scale-dependence of anneal ROI beyond restating Llama-3's own sentence.
- **Assumption: the eight data types span mid-training.** → Hides **multilingual entirely** (not a category, not a Figure 1 icon, not a Table II column) and **agentic entirely** (zero occurrences; only `Agent-FLAN` as a MAP-Neo instruction dataset). For V5's Indic ≥12% and agentic ≥2% floors: there is no cross-run precedent to cite for either lane's share in a mid-train mix. We are the first to write it down; say so rather than implying support.
- **Assumption: "high quality" is a scalar the primaries agree on.** → Hides that the two disclosed selectors are incompatible: Hunyuan-Large keeps the top **5%** by quality; OpenCoder keeps **84%** of the *original distribution* for continuity. For V5: the OPUS selector's ~40% acceptance is a compromise between a quality quantile and a continuity constraint, and the README should name both objectives, not just "quality".
- **Assumption: the anneal should upsample the *best* data.** → Hides that when a low-resource language lane exists, the disclosed behaviour is to *cut* it: MAP-Neo goes from "60% English, 25% Chinese, 15% code" in the general stage to "74.5% English, 8.5% Chinese, 17% code" in mid-training — Chinese is cut ~3× exactly where our preset raises Indic from 16% to 28%. The only counter-precedent is Yi-Lightning's annealing stage with "emphasis on complex reasoning and **low-resource multilingual support**" (§III-B), and Yi-Lightning discloses no percentages. For V5: our Indic-up anneal has exactly one qualitative precedent and one quantitative counter-example. Cite Yi-Lightning by name and expect the MAP-Neo attack.
- **Assumption: synthetic data is safely additive.** → §III-C says "Synthetic data substantially enhances training efficiency and generalization" with no ceiling, no collapse discussion, and no citation to any model-collapse or rephrase-fraction literature. This directly contradicts what we already established (rephrase best ~30%, always <50%; invented textbooks ≤3%; fully synthetic self-training collapses). For V5's Indic tier D (synthetic, 15% of the Indic lane): do not lean on this survey for a synthetic ceiling — it has none.
- **Assumption: token counts are the right unit.** → Hides packing, epoching and batch composition almost completely. The strings `packing`, `epoch` and `batch size` appear essentially nowhere in the data section; the only epoch statement in the whole survey is Zamba's "most datasets were trained for one epoch, while select high-quality subsets were upsampled for two." For V5's repeat budgets (Indic/reason/long at ~1.2×, code/web/STEM covered): the survey supports "a few times, diminishing returns" and nothing sharper.

## Implementation nits

- **Objective:** unchanged next-token CE throughout mid-training. Only FIM changes the sampling of prediction targets, and only DeepSeek-V3 uses it in mid-training (Table II, Fig. 1). §III-A: FIM "does not compromise the next-token prediction capability".
- **Optimizers:** AdamW nearly universally in Table III (Adam for GPT-3, Gopher, MiniCPM; Adafactor for PaLM; Zamba uses "Zero-1 distributed"). Chinchilla's finding that AdamW > Adam at scale is quoted as the reason.
- **Warmup:** linear, ubiquitous. DeepSeek/DeepSeek-V2 use a **2K-step linear warmup** then discrete drops at fixed milestones. Phi-4 (commented source) had **batch size 5760, weight decay 0.1, peak LR 3e-4**. Gopher warms from **1e-7**.
- **Decay depth:** LLaMA 1/2 and Qwen decay to **10% of peak**; LLaMA 3 decays to **1% of peak** in the main leg then **linearly to 0** over the final 40M tokens; SmolLM2/SmolLM3 (WSD) go **straight to 0**. §IV-D notes linear-to-zero may beat cosine (Defazio 2310.07831; Bergsma 2502.15938).
- **Sequence length by stage:** AFM **4k → 8k (anneal) → 32k**; SmolLM2 **2k → 8k** over 75B; Llama-3 **8K → 128K** in 6 stages over ~800B, anneal held at 128K; Qwen3 **4K (S1 and S2) → 32K → 128K** at inference; MiMo **8K → 32K → 64K**; DeepSeek-V3 **4K → 32K → 128K**, 1000 steps per phase; Hunyuan-Large **4K → 32K → 256K**; Hunyuan-A13B **4K → 8K (fast anneal) → 32K → 256K**; Pangu **32K held through the reasoning and both annealing phases**.
- **RoPE bases:** InternLM2 50K→1M; SmolLM2 10k→130k; Phi-4 ~250K; MiMo 10K→64K; Gemma 3 global layers 10k→1M (local unchanged); Qwen-2.5 10k→1M; Qwen-2.5-1M up to 10M; SmolLM3 1.5M→5M. Survey default: **θ ≈ 10⁶ for ≤128k**.
- **Packing:** not discussed anywhere. The only adjacent statement is Phi-4's (commented) finding that inherently-long documents beat samples "padded together to fill the sequence", and the rendered §III-C version: "naturally long-form content outperforms artificially concatenated data in long-context reasoning."
- **Epochs / repetition:** Zamba — most datasets 1 epoch, select high-quality subsets 2. §III-C — repeat high-value data "a few times", diminishing returns past a point. No epoch ceilings given.
- **Checkpoint arithmetic:** OLMo-2 averages **3–4 independently-ordered mid-training runs**; Llama-3 **Polyak-averages checkpoints during annealing**. Both are cheap, both are directly usable.
- **Proxy models:** Pangu ablates data strategies with a **7B proxy** and curriculum-based sampling.
- **Code links:** the survey ships none — no repo, no released mixture configs, no scripts. It is prose plus 7 tables.
- **Local artifacts:** `research/papers/midtrain-survey/{html.raw.html, src.tar.gz, tex/, 1_overview.png, 2_lrs.png, 4_Relative_Frequency_Scaling.png, fig1_hi-1.png, fig1_overview_full.png, fig1_left.png, fig1_right.png, fig1_tile1.png … fig1_tile4.png}`.

## Assignment mapping

**1. Budget share for every capability lane (code, agentic, reasoning, long-context, Indic, STEM, general web; sum 100%) — PARTIALLY SUPPORTS, and challenges the shape of our table.**
Supports four of seven lanes with named splits. Best quotes: SmolLM2's anneal — *"The annealing stage adds 1T high-quality tokens (58% web, 24% code, 14% math, 4% synthetic textbooks)"* (§III-B) — and Llama-3's run mix — *"50% general knowledge, 17% code, 25% math/reasoning, and 8% multilingual"* (§III-B), MAP-Neo's *"74.5% English, 8.5% Chinese, 17% code"*, Nemotron-4's *"70% English, 15% multilingual, and 15% code"*, MiMo's *"increases mathematics and code data to about 70%"*, MiniMax-M1's *"STEM, code, books, and reasoning-related content constitute 70% of the corpus."* Challenges the *form* of our table in two ways: no surveyed run publishes a 7-lane partition (4 lanes is the maximum disclosed), and **no run treats long-context as a lane percentage** — it is always a separate stage with its own window and its own 0.1–8% budget. Our 24/2/6/6/16/12/34 is finer-grained than anything in the field, so every lane needs its own justification; the survey supplies one for code (17–24%), math/STEM (10–14% flat, up to 70% in a math-heavy anneal) and web (50–74% main, 58% anneal).

**2. Indic split across verified / unverified / translated / synthetic — SILENT, and structurally so.**
Not one surveyed run discloses a quality tiering of any language lane. The nearest statements are MAP-Neo's bare Chinese share (25% → 8.5%) and the generic *"synthetic textbook-style and knowledge-dense data significantly enhance LLMs… particularly in low-resource domains such as math, coding, and multilingual contexts"* (§III-A) — which endorses synthetic multilingual data with no fraction, no ceiling and no quality distinction. Our A/B/C/D = 40/25/20/15 has no support and no contradiction here; it must be defended from the Indic inventory and from Demystifying-Synthetic / Model-Collapse, not from this survey.

**3. Agentic, reasoning, long-context as first-class pretrain slots — SUPPORTS reasoning and long-context; SILENT on agentic (the strongest negative finding in the paper).**
Reasoning: *"Using reasoning data during mid-training has become a trend"* (§III-A); Qwen3's second stage is **5T** tokens with "increased STEM, coding, reasoning, and synthetic data" and Pangu's is a named **3T reasoning phase** with "synthetic short- and long-form CoT samples"; 7 of the last 9 models in Figure 1 carry the reasoning icon. Long-context: a dedicated stage in 11 of 17 Table II rows, with the survey's own aggregate rule *"expand the window progressively (e.g., 4k→32k→128k), anneal the learning rate, and mix long/short tokens (often 30–60% long)"* (§V-D). Agentic: **zero**. The string `agent` occurs exactly once in the entire source, as `Agent-FLAN` inside MAP-Neo's instruction column of Table I. Figure 1's eight-icon legend has no agentic category. Our agentic pretrain slot is genuinely novel — the README should claim that rather than imply precedent, and should borrow the FIM justification (*"FIM increases data efficiency by allowing multiple span-prediction opportunities from a single sample, making it well-suited for late-stage training where high-quality data is scarce"*, §III-A) as the mechanism for stretching 0.63B unique agentic tokens.

**4. Protected always-on floor the OPUS selector cannot starve — SUPPORTS the principle with no number; CHALLENGES it for the multilingual lane.**
Support: *"OLMo 2 finds that even a small fraction of domain-specific data (e.g., math and coding) within the mid-training mix can produce disproportionate performance gains… it is not strictly necessary to have a large proportion of domain-specific data in the annealing mixture, just that domain-specific data is present"* (§III-C) — that is precisely the floor argument, and it says a small floor is enough, which supports our 8%-of-accepted-tokens always-on channel. Second support: *"OpenCoder retains 84% of its mid-training data from the original pretraining distribution"* (§III-C) — a continuity floor, i.e. the selector is not allowed to starve the base distribution either. Challenge: the one disclosed case of a low-resource lane meeting a quality-driven mid-train mix is MAP-Neo, where **Chinese falls 25% → 8.5%** — a quality-first selector *does* starve the low-resource lane unless a floor is imposed. That is the best available argument for hard-coding the Indic floor rather than trusting OPUS.

**5. Anneal reserve — which best data is held back for the cooldown — STRONGLY SUPPORTS, with the sharpest number in the paper.**
*"During the learning rate annealing phase, the model is trained on 5% of the highest-quality pre-training tokens, which plays a pivotal role in augmenting the model's performance"* (Hunyuan-Large, §III-B) — a top-5% quality quantile is the reserve rule. Composition precedents: SmolLM2 **58/24/14/4**; Zamba **60% old / 40% new** across >100 curated sources; Pangu **+20% instruction, +18% advanced STEM**; MiMo **~70% math+code**; OLMo-2 Dolmino-Mix-1124 (DCLM-baseline, peS2o, Wiki, CodeSearchNet-OWM, MetaMath-OWM, GSM8K-train, FLAN, StackExchange, TuluMath, Dolmino SynthMath, TinyGSM-MIND, MathCoder2-synthetic); Llama-3 "upsample data sources of very high quality" + Polyak averaging. Also supports starting specialization in the cooldown rather than in SFT: *"MiniCPM shows that introducing such data earlier in mid-training is more effective than deferring it to fine-tuning"* (§III-C). Constrains us: the reserve is an overlay of **10–40%** on a 60–90% continuity base, so our anneal preset's 32% mass shift is defensible but at the aggressive end.

**6. Each lane tied to the benchmarks it is meant to win — SUPPORTS.**
Table VI is a ready-made lane→benchmark map with shot counts: code → HumanEval 0-shot, MBPP 3-shot, MultiPL-E 0-shot; reasoning/STEM → GSM8K 4-shot CoT, MATH 4-shot CoT, GPQA 5-shot CoT, BBH 3-shot CoT; long-context → LongBench, RULER (+NIAH, HELMET in §V-C); **Indic → MGSM 8-shot CoT, Flores-101 5-shot** (the paper's only multilingual benchmarks); general web → MMLU 5-shot, MMLU-Pro 5-shot CoT, ARC, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA. Quantified lane→benchmark causality: *"FineMath4+ achieves a 2x improvement on GSM8K and a 6x improvement on MATH compared to InfiMM-WebMath"* (§III-C), and OLMo-2's GSM8K **+43.4 (7B) / +37.8 (13B)** from mid-training (Table VII). No agentic benchmark appears anywhere — our agentic lane has no benchmark hook in this literature and needs one named from our own inventory.

**7. Demand vs unique token supply; where a share only works by repeating or synthesizing — MOSTLY SILENT, three partial supports.**
The survey never does supply accounting — no corpus sizes, no unique-token budgets (the dataset token counts that existed in the draft, e.g. "DCLM-baseline 3.8T", "Stack-Edu 125B", "RefineCode 960B", were deleted from the published version and survive only as comments in `2_data.tex`). Three usable partials: (a) *"Moderate repetition and rewriting of high-quality data can substitute for scale… models trained on smaller repeated datasets can outperform those trained on larger, unrepeated datasets, especially in mathematical reasoning"* (§III-C) — licenses our ~1.2× repeats on Indic/reason/long; (b) FIM as a per-sample multiplier for "late-stage training where high-quality data is scarce" (§III-A) — the one mechanism offered for a 63× shortfall; (c) *"synthetic corpora can be tailored to emphasize underrepresented domains"* (§III-C) — endorses synthesis with **no ceiling whatsoever**, which we must not lean on given what Demystifying-Synthetic (<50%, best ~30%) and Model-Collapse already established. Counter-pressure from the other direction: *"Overtraining beyond a critical token budget may degrade downstream tunability"* (§III-C, Springer et al. 2503.19206) — repeating to fill a 2T budget is not free.

## What not to overclaim

- **Do not cite this survey as evidence for any number.** It contains no experiments, no ablations and no reproductions; every quantity is transcribed from a primary, and I verified two transcription errors (LLaMA3-405B annealing "1 (40B)" in Table II vs the correct **40M tokens** in its own §III-B/§IV-C and in Llama 3 2407.21783 §3.4.3; Granite 3.0's Stage-1 mixture summing to **60%**). Use Table II as an index, then cite the primary arXiv ID. Equally: several of the most quotable numbers (OLMo-2's 61/66/65 math-duplication curve, MiniMax-01's linear source-weight interpolation, Phi-4's organic>synthetic finding, DeepSeek-V3's YaRN hyperparameters) exist **only as commented-out LaTeX in the arXiv source** and are absent from the published paper — quoting them as "the survey reports" would be false.
- **Do not claim the survey licenses a large anneal uplift at 120B.** It reports the opposite where scale is measured: *"Annealing improved Llama 3 8B on GSM8k by 24.0% and MATH by 6.4%, but a negligible uplift for 405B model"* (Table VII). OLMo-2's +10.6/+10.3, MiniCPM4's 22%-of-tokens, Gemma2-9B's ~10% and SmolLM2's mixtures are all ≤14B. The 10–50% "best 40%" second-stage sizing we inherited from Two-Phase was also established at small scale; nothing here extends it to 120B, and the mid-train share of a run is *not* observed to be scale-invariant.
- **Do not claim any support for Indic or agentic shares, floors, or tiering.** Multilingual is absent from the eight-type data taxonomy and from Figure 1's legend; agentic is absent from the paper entirely. The two multilingual data points point opposite ways — MAP-Neo cuts Chinese 25%→8.5% entering mid-training, while Yi-Lightning's annealing stage claims "emphasis on complex reasoning and low-resource multilingual support" with no percentages at all. Our Indic-heavy anneal (16%→28%) has exactly one qualitative precedent and one quantitative counter-example, and our agentic pretrain slot has none. Say that plainly in the README instead of implying the field agrees.
