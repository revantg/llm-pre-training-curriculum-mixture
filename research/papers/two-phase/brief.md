## Teach this first

Two-phase pretraining is not a tiny cooldown. Phase-1 (𝒫₁) is most of the run on a *diversity* blend: lots of web crawl (high + medium + some low), medium-quality books/papers/CC derivatives, and only a little math/code/wiki. Phase-2 (𝒫₂) is a *different* blend for a long final stretch: high-quality crawl only, lots of math and code, wiki, and a sliver of task data (FLAN + synthetic GSM8K). A cosine learning-rate decay is layered on top of the blend switch. This is the closest published analog to V5's "main pretrain vs anneal" split, and it is the wrong analog if you treat their 𝒫₂ as V5's ~2% reserved HQ slice — their 𝒫₂ is 10–50% of the run, best at **40%**.

Order is a real lever, not just weights. They hold per-source sampling weights fixed and only change the *order* data is seen: random order (BASE-RO) vs two-phase. Two-phase wins +3.4 average accuracy, almost entirely on GSM8K (+9.5 abs: 30.86 → 40.33) and code (+2.8 abs: 35.55 → 38.33). MMLU is essentially flat (56.49 vs 56.28). So late upsampling of quality data disproportionately helps sharp capability islands (math, code), not smoothly-learned general knowledge. That is the save-and-spend argument for reserving reasoning/code-like data — but it is evidence for a *long* second phase, not for a 2% tail.

They separate two variables labs usually mash together: **source quality** and **how many epochs a source may repeat**. Recipe: (1) pick a token budget and downsample every source by f=1/15 so a 1T prototype preserves relative scarcity of a 15T target; (2) label quality (5-bucket CC classifier, plus a 50B continue-train probe for books/papers/CC_dv); (3) measure the epoch ceiling before other benchmarks drop; (4) spend those epochs thin in 𝒫₁ and concentrated in 𝒫₂. High-quality crawl saturates at 6 epochs (MMLU 56.1, then 55.7 at 8). Math in 𝒫₂ is an inverted-U peaking at 8 epochs (avg 52.28). That is a usable ceiling for *organic* repetition. It is not a license for V5's agentic 63× synthesis, which this paper never tested.

Quality-gating web crawl beats "keep everything for coverage." A natural-distribution CC blend is 64.44% Low-quality tokens (Table 16). Weighted sampling that zeros Low and upsamples High/Medium-High lifts MMLU 42.94 → 56.10 (Table 17). Among four CC blends, the most aggressive drop of Medium-Low/Low (CC-Blend1: 57% High / 25% Medium-High / 18% Medium / 0% tail) wins (Table 11). 𝒫₂ then uses *only* High crawl. This challenges any V5 instinct to dump unverified web or Indic crawl into the mixture for unique-token coverage.

The duration ablation is the number that attacks V5's ~2% anneal. Same winning blend, 𝒫₂ share 0/10/20/30/40/50% of 1T: avg 37.68 / 50.48 / 51.58 / 52.28 / **52.86** / 51.96 (Table 14). Almost all of the gain appears by 10% (especially GSM8K 16.60 → 33.13 and code 16.22 → 32.55). 40% is best; 50% degrades, which they call overfitting on the smaller quality pool. They never tested anything between 0 and 10%. So this paper *challenges* treating 2% as an "anneal" in their sense, *supports* "hold back best data for the end," and is *silent* on whether a 2% low-LR cooldown on top of a longer mid-training phase would work.

When you scale tokens without re-capping epochs, the blend itself must change. Naively reusing 𝒫₂-Blend1 from 1T → 1.7T (same downsampled pool, so every source sees 1.7× more epochs) scores 53.28 avg. 𝒫₂-Blend6, which cuts math 24→14.4, code 20→12, task 1.3→0.8 and pumps crawl 31→49 to keep high-quality epochs near the 8-epoch ceiling, scores 54.45 (+2.2 vs the 1T run, +1.17 vs the naive 1.7T run; Table 8 / Table 15). The 15T full-data run keeps epoch counts constant (no extra repeats) and jumps 52.86 → 59.84. V5's 1–10T ladder has to decide which of those two scaling modes it is in.

Task data in 𝒫₂ is tiny and late, and it works at that dose. FLAN + FLAN-CoT + synthetic GSM8K-train is 1.3% of every 𝒫₂ blend. Appendix D: adding FLAN (10 or 20 epochs) lifts reasoning and GSM8K without hurting MMLU; adding synthetic GSM8K-train jumps GSM8K to 35.56; combining everything (Combo) hits avg 50.27 / GSM8K 45.19. This is evidence that synthetic instruction/reasoning tokens belong in the *late* slice, at trace concentration. It is not evidence that a majority-synthetic agentic lane is safe.

## Method, in order

1. **Fix a 15T target and downsample.** Assume Llama-3-like 15T final budget. Set f = 1/15 so a 1T prototype sees exactly 1/15 of each source's tokens. Relative scarcity (wiki 16.7B, task data 6.6B vs crawl 6244.3B; Table 1) is preserved so epoch decisions transfer when you later train 15T on the *full* pool at the same epoch counts.
2. **Label web-crawl quality.** Run every Common Crawl document (99 snapshots) through the Su et al. 2024 Nemotron-CC 5-way classifier: High / Medium-High / Medium / Medium-Low / Low.
3. **Build a weighted-sampling crawl blend.** Upsample High and Medium-High; keep some Medium; drop or minimize Medium-Low/Low. Compare to natural distribution (Table 16/17). Sweep four CC blends that trade High for tail (Table 10); pick CC-Blend1 (57 / 25 / 18 / 0 / 0) via Table 11.
4. **Probe the other medium sources.** Continue-train the 𝒫₁-Blend4 checkpoint 50B tokens on 66% test-source + 34% CC-High. Books, papers, and CC_dv land near CC-Medium-High, below CC-High (Table 12). Group them as medium-quality: allowed in 𝒫₁, not upsampled into 𝒫₂. Dedicated high-quality sources (math/code/wiki) beat CC-High on average.
5. **Measure per-source epoch ceilings.** Hold overall crawl % fixed, sweep 1/2/4/6/8 epochs of high-quality crawl inside 𝒫₁; evaluate MMLU (Figure 4). Separately sweep 1/4/8/12 epochs of math and of task data inside 𝒫₂ (Table 13). Recommendation they print: 6 epochs high-quality crawl, 8 epochs math and task data.
6. **Assemble five 𝒫₁ blends and five 𝒫₂ blends** (Tables 2–3) that each move one axis (code vs medium, crawl vs medium, crawl vs high-quality, math-heavy). 𝒫₁ encourages diversity (crawl 58–70%, math only 1.9–2.9%, no task data). 𝒫₂ emphasizes quality (crawl High-only 31–40%, math 24–29%, code 20–29%, wiki 1%, task data 1.3%, multilingual held at 3.7%).
7. **Kill 𝒫₁ blends on a held-out validation split.** Train 8B / 1T on all five. Blend5 and Blend2 sit 2.8% and 2.1% higher in validation loss than Blend4 at ~250B tokens (Figure 2) and are stopped. Remaining three stay within 1% loss, so they switch to downstream eval at 200 / 250 / 629B (Table 6). Blend3 is consistently worst on average; kill it after 629B. Blend4 (highest high-quality share) wins every checkpoint.
8. **Cross surviving 𝒫₁ × every 𝒫₂** (10 combos; Table 7) with the switch at ≈70% of training (last 30% is 𝒫₂). Best: 𝒫₁-Blend4 → 𝒫₂-Blend1, avg 52.28. Any 𝒫₂ switch beats continuing 𝒫₁ for all metrics. Largest absolute gains vs 𝒫₁-Blend4-only: GSM8K +14.6, code +9.4.
9. **Sweep 𝒫₂ duration** 0/10/20/30/40/50% on the winning combo (Table 14). 40% is best (avg 52.86); 50% degrades (51.96). Headline Table 4 / Table 5 1T numbers are this 40% run, not the 30% Table 7 run.
10. **Pick the 𝒫₂ LR shape** (Appendix E, Table 29) at a fixed 10% 𝒫₂: linear vs cosine, floor 0 vs 3e-6 (1% of 𝒫₁ peak 3e-4). Negligible differences. Final: cosine decay to 3e-6 so post-training still has headroom. 𝒫₁ itself uses cosine from 3e-4 targeted at min-LR 3e-6 over the *full* two-phase run; 𝒫₂ starts from the intermediate LR at the switch (Appendix A).
11. **Scale three ways**, holding 𝒫₁-Blend4→𝒫₂-Blend1 unless noted: (a) more tokens on the *same downsampled* pool, 1T→1.7T — must re-cap epochs via 𝒫₂-Blend6 (Table 8); (b) full (not downsampled) data at 15T, 8B — epochs constant, avg 52.86→59.84 (Table 5); (c) 25B params at 1T, seq len 8192 — avg 53.92→58.47, validation loss still falling (Table 9, Figure 3).

## Assumptions

- **Global token budget is known in advance (15T) so f=1/15 is well-defined.** → V5's 1T→10T ladder is only the same trick if source *proportions* stay fixed. You cannot downsample agentic/Indic data you do not have yet; synthesis is a different operation than f=1/15.
- **Quality is a document-level English-CC property (5-bucket Nemotron-CC), or a whole-dataset label (books/papers/CC_dv).** → Silent on Indic crawl. An English quality classifier will over- or under-fire on low-resource text; "drop Low/Medium-Low" is not a verified Indic rule.
- **Epoch ceilings (6 crawl, ~8 math/task) are a fixed integer, not a function of unique-token supply.** → Derived on sources with tens to hundreds of billions of unique tokens (math 161.5B, code 760.3B). Agentic at 0.63B unique vs 40B 2T-demand is already ~63 epochs if you only repeat — far past anything they measured.
- **One global phase switch at a single token boundary.** → Not a per-lane schedule. V5's "reserve long-CoT / long-agent now, spend later" is a finer claim than this paper tests.
- **Synthetic data is a ≤1.3% (or at most a few percent in Appendix D) curated task-data add-on, not a majority lane.** → Positive GSM8K/FLAN result does not transfer to majority-synthetic agentic volume.
- **The "hold back best data" pool is large (whole 𝒫₂ = 30–40% of the run) and English-benchmarked.** → Smallest tested 𝒫₂ is 10%. Cannot support or refute a 2% reserve; can only say their own objective wanted ≥10%, likely ~40%.
- **Multilingual is a flat 9-language Wikipedia+CC blob, 3.7–5%.** → No Indic, no verified/unverified/translated/synthetic split, no language-level quality filter.
- **Downstream suite is MMLU / commonsense reasoning / GSM8K / HumanEval+MBPP.** → No agentic, long-context, Indic, or multilingual eval. Optimal 40% could move if those enter the objective.

## Implementation nits

- Megatron-LM causal decoder, Tiktoken, RoPE, RMSNorm, GQA. No public blend configs or data-pipeline repo (proprietary NVIDIA stack; Megatron-LM itself is open).
- Adam β=(0.9, 0.95), ε=1e-08, global batch 1536, seq 4096 default / 8192 for the 8B-vs-25B pair, ≤1024 H100.
- CC quality labels consumed from Su et al. 2024 Nemotron-CC (arXiv 2412.02595); this paper does not report the classifier's k, proxy size, or non-English validation.
- Blend search is grid + early-stop: 5 𝒫₁ candidates, kill 2 on val-loss at ~250B, kill 1 more on downstream at 629B, then 2×5 𝒫₂ cross. Cheap proxy trick worth copying for V5 ablations.
- Task data = FLAN + FLAN-CoT (Longpre et al. 2023) + synthetically generated GSM8K-train (data-augmentation style, Feng et al. 2021) + SFT-style alignment data cited via Toshniwal et al. 2024 / Nemotron-4. Not a Phi-style model-generated corpus.
- High-quality sources named: math (Paster et al. 2024 OpenWebMath-class + StackExchange), code (Li et al. 2023 StarCoder), Wikipedia. Medium: books & patents, papers (Gao et al. 2020 The Pile), CC_dv = OpenWebText, BigScience, Reddit, CC-News.
- Multilingual: "9 languages derived from Wikipedia and Common Crawl." Languages never listed.
- Appendix D 𝒫₂ probes use a 900B 𝒫₁ checkpoint (10% 𝒫₂) and linear LR to 0; those avgs are **not** comparable to the main 40% cosine tables (code avg there excludes HumanEval+/MBPP+).

## Follow-on papers

- **Su et al. 2024, Nemotron-CC (arXiv 2412.02595)** · 5-bucket CC quality classifier this paper depends on. Changes a V5 web (and maybe Indic) number if we adopt document-level quality gating — only after checking non-English validation.
- **Blakeney et al. 2024, "Does your data spark joy?" (arXiv 2406.03476)** · End-of-training domain upsampling; the closest prior to a *short* anneal. Their duration is the calibration point for V5's ~2%, because this paper never tested below 10%.
- **Xie et al. 2023a, DoReMi (NeurIPS 2023)** · Proxy-model mixture optimization. This paper explicitly does *not* do that (hand grid). Relevant if V5 wants algorithmic floors instead of composer hand-weights.
- **Li et al. 2023, StarCoder / The Stack** · The code source behind their 760.3B code bucket. Changes the V5 code-lane unique-token supply and repeat math.
- **Parmar et al. 2024a, "Data, data everywhere" (ACL 2024)** · Same group's dataset-construction guide; likely has licensing/provenance this paper omits.
- **Dubey et al. 2024, Llama 3 (arXiv 2407.21783)** · The 15T target this paper copies. Llama 3's own (mostly undisclosed) end-of-training upsample is the other industry analog for anneal length.

## Assignment mapping

1. **Budget share for every capability lane (code / agentic / reason / long-ctx / Indic / STEM / web, sum 100%) — silent on the 7-lane cut; usable only as a coarse analog.** Their categories are crawl / math / wiki / code / books / papers / CC_dv / multilingual / task data. No agentic, no long-context. Approximate mapping of the *winning* blends: 𝒫₁-Blend4 is web-heavy (59% crawl + 5% multilingual + 13% books/papers/CC_dv ≈ 77% "web-ish"), code 20%, STEM/math 2.9%, reasoning-shaped task data 0%. 𝒫₂-Blend1 is crawl 31% + multilingual 3.7% + medium 19% ≈ 54% web-ish, math 24%, code 20%, task data 1.3%. V5 main preset 24/2/6/6/16/12/34 and anneal 20/8/18/8/28/10/8 do not match either phase. Quote: "In phase-1 (𝒫₁), we explore a general data distribution which consists of a mix of web crawl data, medium-quality data, and low amounts of high-quality data. In phase-2 (𝒫₂), we explore a blend which includes task data and emphasizes high-quality datasets such as math, code, and high-quality web crawl" (§2).

2. **Indic split (verified / unverified / translated / synthetic) — silent.** "Multilingual: Multilingual data (9 languages) derived from Wikipedia and Common Crawl." (§3.1). Flat 5.0% in every 𝒫₁ blend, 3.7% in every 𝒫₂ blend (4.2% only in the 1.7T Blend6). No language list, no quality split, no translation/synthetic Indic.

3. **Agentic / reasoning / long-context as first-class named slots — challenges by omission.** Reasoning is an *eval* bucket (CommonsenseQA, ARC, PIQA, …) plus GSM8K, not a training lane. Agentic trajectories and long-context packs do not exist. The only reasoning-shaped training data is "Task Data" at 1.3% of 𝒫₂: "This includes data used for supervised finetuning (SFT) during the alignment phase … We also include the FLAN collection." (§3.1). Even this well-resourced run treats reasoning-shaped data as a hand-curated add-on, not a first-class pretrain slot with inventory.

4. **Protected always-on floor OPUS cannot starve (Indic + agentic + reasoning) — silent / weak indirect hint only.** No floor mechanism; blends are static percentages from a grid. Multilingual is the only category held nearly constant across phases (5% → 3.7%), which is a weak analog of "don't starve the scarce language lane when you switch to quality," but they never argue a floor and they *do* drop multilingual 1.3 points into 𝒫₂. Agentic/reasoning have no always-on share at all (task data is 𝒫₂-only).

5. **Anneal reserve: which best data is held back for cooldown — supports the *idea*, challenges the *2% number*.** Held back for 𝒫₂: High-only crawl (not Medium/Medium-High), all of math, wiki, code, and task data, for the last **≈40%** of training, with LR annealed to 3e-6. Quote: "Phase-two for the last ≈40% of training yields the best results, and over-extending it leads to diminishing returns." (§7). Table 14: 10% already captures most of the GSM8K/code jump (16.60→33.13 GSM8K, 16.22→32.55 code); 0% is a collapse on those two; 50% overfits. V5's ~2% reserved HQ low-LR slice is 5× smaller than their smallest tested 𝒫₂ and 20× smaller than their optimum. Two different objects: their 𝒫₂ is a second pretraining *blend* lasting hundreds of billions of tokens; V5's 2% is a cooldown. The paper supports "save best data for the end + decay LR," not "2% is enough."

6. **Each lane tied to the benchmarks it is meant to win — partially supports.** They explicitly attach math/code/task-data upsampling to GSM8K and HumanEval/MBPP, and high-quality crawl epochs to MMLU (Figure 4). Quote: "tasks such as code and math are sensitive to the ordering of high-quality data in the second phase" (§4); "largest absolute gains in GSM8K and code of 14.6% and 9.4%" (§4.1). No agentic→benchmark or long-context→benchmark or Indic→benchmark link exists.

7. **Demand vs unique-token supply; where a share only works by repeating or synthesizing — supports.** Epoch ceilings are the transferable number: organic high-quality crawl saturates at 6, math/task at ~8, and going to 12 hurts the average (Table 13). Scaling 1T→1.7T on the *same* unique pool without re-capping epochs underperforms an epoch-adjusted blend (Table 8). Natural inventory (Table 1) already shows the scarcity shape: task data 6.6B and wiki 16.7B vs crawl 6.2T. Directly relevant to V5: OPUS ~6× effective tokens is inside their tested single-digit-epoch band; agentic ~63× is not, so that share only works by *synthesizing variation*, not by repeating unique tokens. Their own synthetic test is GSM8K-train at 2% of an Appendix-D 𝒫₂ blend — not 63×.

## What not to overclaim

- **Do not quote 40% as the right V5 anneal.** It was tuned on English MMLU/GSM8K/code with no Indic, agentic, or long-context objective, and the search grid starts at 10%. The 0→10% jump is the robust finding; the 30 vs 40 vs 50 ranking is a 1-point-avg wiggle with no error bars.
- **Two-phase here is one global switch, not V5's per-lane choreography.** Nothing tests staggering agentic later than reasoning, or holding Indic at a floor while STEM anneals. Using this paper to bless "Seed → General → Reasoning → Long-ctx → Anneal" as a sequence is an inference, not a result.
- **The 8-epoch ceiling is not a synthesis budget.** It is an *organic-repeat* ceiling on large unique pools. Citing it to justify 63× agentic generation, or to claim collapse begins at epoch 8, is out of scope. Appendix D's happy synthetic-GSM8K result is a few percent of 𝒫₂, not a majority lane.
